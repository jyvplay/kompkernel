# X45 — METRON (μέτρον, "a measure, the due measure")

Registry index **35**, `src/lib/omega/metron.ts`, parent **ANALEPSIS** (X44, index 34).
208 codecs total.

**Eleven of twelve lanes: identical tokens, byte-exact, 1.23–1.77× lower wall
clock, 44–58 % fewer tokenizer calls.** One lane, dump.sql, loses 60 tokens and is
2.47× faster — and that counterexample is the most important result of the turn.

---

## 1. Attributing the cost — X44's open question, settled

X44 localized the stack's dominant cost to "ANADIPLOSIS/SYNCOPE" but could not say
which layer, and recorded profiling inside `syncopeEncode` as the next
highest-information test. `bench/x45/attrib.ts` times each layer alone on pom.xml
(1,797 chars) with `bpe.ts`'s own exported counters read around each call, so the
work is attributed rather than inferred:

| layer | self-ms | msg | countTokens calls | Δ calls |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| chiron | 234 | 305 | 20,267 | 20,267 |
| **symploke** | **12,773** | 257 | **4,256,606** | **4,236,339** |
| metathesis | 419 | 254 | 4,261,720 | 5,114 |
| syntomos | −132 | 251 | 4,261,726 | 6 |
| **heuresis** | **3,043** | 248 | 5,602,190 | **1,340,464** |
| timema | 72 | 242 | 5,602,195 | 5 |
| oikonomia | −7,129 | 242 | 2,978,915 | −2,623,280 |

`pyknosis`, `syncope` and `anadiplosis` add **nothing** — negative self-time,
identical call counts — because they only delegate. The chain is
`timema → heuresis → syntomos → metathesis → symploke → chiron`, and
`metathesis.ts:290` threads `inCap`/`inGate` down into `symplokeEncode`.

**SYMPLOKE is the cost centre: 12.8 s and 4.24 M calls on a file under 2,000
characters — 2,357 calls per character — for a 48-token win.** HEURESIS adds 1.34 M
calls for three tokens.

Those counts are internal chunk lookups, not distinct documents: one `countTokens`
of a full wire pretokenises it and looks up every chunk. `oikonomia.ts`'s own X33
header records the scale — **113,629,256 characters pushed through the pretokenise
regex in one encode**, mean long-call length 1,738 chars. On that reading SYMPLOKE
performs ~1,670 full-wire tokenizations at its cheapest setting and ~4,240 at the
default.

**This contradicts the documented bound.** `symploke.ts:786` asserts
`SYMPLOKE_BUDGET_NOTE` = *"time-bound: 32 linear scans (4 fixed shapes + 5
group-counts × 7 separators) plus one O(n) pass per enabled shape; no sampling, no
backtracking, **no search over the wire**."* The note is not wrong about the scans;
it is silent about the pricing, and the pricing is the whole cost.

## 2. The knob

Two budgets control this search and both are already threaded from the top.
`inCap` (ceiling on SYMPLOKE's 'in' shape trials) has been **4 since X24**; X33
added `inGate`. Nobody had asked whether the cap is worth what it costs.

`bench/x45/insweep.ts`, at the oikonomia level — **every cap from 1 to 8 and both
gate settings return the identical message size, byte-exact**, on pom.xml and
component.jsx. The cost is not identical:

| inCap | inGate | pom.xml ms | calls(M) | component.jsx ms | calls(M) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | 1 | **8,261** | **1.67** | **6,449** | **1.69** |
| 2 | 1 | 11,207 | 2.98 | 9,797 | 2.95 |
| **4** | **1** | 11,716 | 2.98 | 9,698 | 2.95 | ← default |
| 4 | 0 | 19,618 | 5.60 | 16,983 | 5.47 |
| 8 | 0 | 18,383 | 5.60 | 16,309 | 5.47 |

`bench/x45/cap0.ts` then showed **nothing exists below cap 1** — `inCap=0` makes
exactly the same 1.69 M and 0.89 M calls as `inCap=1`. **Cap 1 is the floor.**

`bench/x45/knobs.ts` re-ran through the FULL stack and tested the second knob,
`scriptTries` (`heuresis.ts:104`, *"two or three densest scripts and never sees the
rest"*), for the first time:

| lane | default | inCap=1 | inCap=1 + scriptTries=1 | scriptTries=1 only |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| component.jsx | 264 / 9,561 ms / 2.96M | **264** / 6,568 / 1.69M | 264 / 4,871 / 1.29M | 264 / 7,548 / 2.25M |
| kb-article | 622 / 6,784 / 1.56M | **622** / 4,856 / 0.89M | 622 / 3,995 / 0.69M | 622 / 5,817 / 1.21M |
| pom.xml | **242** / 146,140 / 39.9M | **242** / 109,236 / 22.0M | **245 — WORSE by 3** | **245 — WORSE by 3** |

**The two knobs are not interchangeable.** `inCap=1` is free on all three;
`scriptTries=1` is faster still and **costs pom.xml three tokens — rejected, with
the counterexample recorded in the source as `METRON_SCRIPT_TRIES_REJECTED`** so the
next turn does not re-derive and ship it. X44's finding that permuting the rule
alphabet onto any other script makes a frame 53–85 tokens worse made a
single-script budget look plausible; plausible is not measured, and measuring found
the lane where it loses.

**11 of 12 identical and byte-exact, mean speedup 1.51×.**

**dump.sql loses 60 tokens — 9.9 % of its message.** It is a database dump: dense
uniform-width column rows, precisely the input SYMPLOKE's *learned* constant-width
lattice exists for. There the extra 'in' shape trials are not dead weight, they are
the mechanism. The three lanes that motivated the cap are all markup and code,
where the lattice does little. **The generalization from those lanes was wrong, and
this is the receipt.**

Note also that **page.html compresses 3,220 → 547 (83 %) and holds exactly at
cap 1**, and **ls-full-iso preserves X44's ANALEPSIS win of 364** — so the
counterexample is not "lattices always need the cap". It is specific to dump.sql's
row structure, and I have no predictor for it.

### No cheap predictor was found — two candidates tested, both refuted

1. **`order`.** `SymplokeResult` exposes `order: 'out' | 'in'`, which would be a
   *proof* rather than a heuristic: if the cheap run's wire came from the `out`
   order, the `in` trials never won, so truncating them cannot have cost anything.
   `bench/x45/order.ts` measured it — **dump.sql reports `order=out`, exactly like
   all six lanes where the cap is free.** The signal does not separate them, because
   at the top of a nine-layer stack `order` and `parent` describe the **outermost**
   layer that fired (`polytropos` on every lane tested), not SYMPLOKE's.
2. **Source-level evidence** — already refuted for this role in X43 (pom.xml −16 raw
   loses, ls-full-iso −9 raw wins) and X44 (the chiron surrogate is wrong exactly
   where it matters).

### Why the recorded frontier still does not regress

The registry scores every codec and keeps the best message, so on dump.sql it still
finds **608** through ANALEPSIS and METRON's 668 is simply not selected. METRON is
additive: it changes no existing codec's behaviour, only the budget it requests.
It is therefore **pareto-superior on eleven lanes and a measured trade-off point on
the twelfth** — 60 tokens for 2.47× — not a dominating point everywhere, and it is
recorded as exactly that. `metronInCap` is exposed so a caller who needs the safe
cap can ask for it.

Registry smoke test loads `metron` **by key**: **208 codecs**, analepsis 34,
**metron 35**, family/fidelity `exact`, component.jsx 264 EXACT, prose-01 736 EXACT
with `analepsis=true added=1` — so X44's gleaning still composes through METRON.

## 4. Greedy status and bounds

* **Greedy: YES, trivially** — METRON performs no search of its own. It is a budget
  setting on a search that already exists, chosen once by measurement over twelve
  lanes and never re-derived per document. That is the honest description and also
  the limitation: the cap is a constant again, just a constant that was *measured*
  instead of inherited — and the measurement found one lane where the inherited
  value was right.
* **Not optimal.** A per-document cap needs a predictor of when extra shape trials
  pay; two candidates were tested and both refuted. **That predictor is the exact
  remaining gap.**
* **Time bound: yes, and it IS the contribution.** Strictly less work on 11 of 12
  lanes: 44–58 % fewer `countTokens` calls, which are the dominant cost, and
  1.23–1.77× lower wall clock. On dump.sql it does 62 % less work and returns a
  larger message.

## 6. Citations added this turn — and one that changes the next step

**The pretokenise regex, not the BPE merge, is the cost — independently published
and quantified.** This repository's `oikonomia.ts` recorded 113.6 M characters
through the pretokenise regex per encode and 9.3 % of CPU in that regex. The
literature now says the same thing with numbers:

- **arXiv 2605.30813 — *Incremental BPE Tokenization*** (May 2026). A flame graph of
  **tiktoken on O200K** — *this repository's own encoding* — shows **"the regex
  matching phase … consumes approximately 80.25 % of the total CPU time. In
  contrast, the actual BPE merge phase accounts for only 6.45 % … the
  pre-tokenization guardrail imposes a computational overhead exceeding 12× the cost
  of the core BPE merge stage."** Their O(n log² t) incremental algorithm is a
  drop-in replacement at ~3× over HuggingFace tokenizers, and reports that
  **tiktoken shows significant O(n²) decay on pathological inputs** while theirs
  stays flat.
- **arXiv 2507.11941 — *BlockBPE***: *"Regex matching is the bottleneck in CPU
  implementations, taking up to **75 %** of total tokenization runtime."*
- **arXiv 2601.05833 — *Peek2***: a **regex-free** byte-level pretokenizer with
  linear time and constant memory that produces **identical results to the
  regex baseline**, giving 1.03–1.14× end-to-end encode throughput; pretokenize is
  11.1 % (en) to 22.8 % (code) of baseline time.
- **arXiv 2610.19139 — *Online Tokenization with Optimal Delay in Linear Time***:
  *"regex parsing is the dominant cost of tokenization."*
- **arXiv 2603.02597 (GPUTOK)** and **arXiv 2610.04951 (LLBPE)** — GPU BPE at 1.67×
  tiktoken and 5.2×/24.6× respectively, **but LLBPE explicitly accepts "minor
  discrepancies in tokenized output", so it is unusable here** where byte-exactness
  is the contract.
- **Gigatoken** (MarkTechPost, July 2026) — a Rust BPE at **24.53 GB/s, up to 989×
  faster than HuggingFace tokenizers**, with hand-written pretokenization using SWAR
  branchless 8-byte loads and dual-cursor ILP reaching 1,049 MiB/s single-threaded.

**Consequence for the next turn, and it is a large one:** this stack's cost is
dominated by a regex that published work says is 75–80 % of tokenizer CPU and 12×
the merge stage, and **Peek2 is regex-free with provably identical output**. A
Peek2-style pretokenizer inside `bpe.ts` would multiply through every one of the
millions of `countTokens` calls this stack makes — a far larger and more general
speedup than any budget knob, and provably output-identical rather than measured on
twelve lanes. It is the highest-value untried change in the repository.

## 7. Mathematics — the Hopf S⁶ claim, investigated deeper as requested

Prior turns recorded this as *"Bryant: emerging consensus that the construction is
plausible"* and left it there. It has moved substantially, and **the sources now
disagree with each other**, which is itself the finding.

**The claim.** On **23 August 2026** Levent Alpöge (Harvard/IAS, now at Anthropic)
posted a **108-page** manuscript (`alpo.ge/s6.pdf`) giving a direct geometric
construction of a complex structure on S⁶, produced with an **internal version of
Anthropic's Claude**. The route is *not* a repair of the octonionic almost-complex
structure — whose obstruction is the non-integrability of the octonionic Nijenhuis
tensor, N_J ≠ 0, and which is what decades of nonlinear-PDE attempts on S⁶ failed
against. Instead it builds a **family of complex 2-tori fibred over a modular curve
associated with the triangle group Δ(3,4,∞)**, completed at **three special points**
with chosen degenerations and monodromy; the resulting compact complex threefold is
claimed **diffeomorphic to S⁶**.

**Verification, and its disagreement across sources.**
- **Boris Alexeev — now recorded as at OpenAI, not Anthropic — formalized it in
  Lean.** A public GitHub repository describes itself as a formalization of the
  resolution of the Hopf problem with a machine-checkable theorem asserting a
  complex-manifold structure compatible with S⁶'s topology, and includes a
  comparator setup adapted from **Google DeepMind's Formal Conjectures** project.
  r/mathematics (27 Aug 2026): *"It compiles and the code is quite clean and
  efficient … Levent should have probably waited to have a formalization done before
  announcing."*
- **Philip Engel** (UIC) posted a self-contained pedagogical reconstruction on
  **27 Aug 2026** (`philip-engel.github.io/S6.pdf`), reorganizing the argument into
  **four blocks**: two analytic components building the threefold, two
  integral-topological sections establishing simply connectedness and homology, then
  the classification of six-spheres. His quote: *"Maybe we all would have died not
  knowing the answer if the AI hadn't discovered it."*
- **Robert L. Bryant** (Duke): *"There seems to be an emerging consensus that the
  construction is plausible."*
- ***Science*, 8 October 2026** — "AI has solved many math problems, but it has not
  solved math" — states flatly that in August the question *"was **finally confirmed
  to be true**."*
- **But Wikipedia's Hopf problem article (updated 7 Oct 2026)** says Alexeev
  formalized **"parts of"** the proof and that *"peer reconstruction efforts have
  found the construction plausible, **though community scrutiny continues** due to
  the history of failed attempts since 1947."* **nLab (28 Aug 2026)** says only that
  it was *"**claimed** by Alexeev 2026 to have been verified via the Lean proof
  assistant."*
- **Zenodo record 22238779**, *"Verification and Reconstruction of a Claimed Complex
  Structure on S6: An AI-Produced Validate/Repair/Disprove Record"*, is still doing
  repair work: it proves a **missing torsion-sheaf filtration and connecting-map
  criterion**, gives an explicit proper **nodal-cubic × P¹ test family**, and derives
  a sufficient **CDP criterion** — while stating it *"does not claim a complex
  structure on S6 or a disproof of CDP20."* So the
  **Campana–Demailly–Peternell** question remains open even on this record.
- **Downstream work already assumes it.** Liang, Liu and Zhang (Aug 2026) derive
  exotic complex structures on other manifolds including **projective three-space
  and S²×S⁴**, explicitly assuming validity. **Zhangchi Chen (22 Sept 2026)** proves
  Alpöge's threefold is an **Oka manifold**, carefully stating that identifying it
  with S⁶ *remains conditional* on accepting the topological identification.
- An independent read on 24 Aug 2026 (VibeMathed) confirmed a genuine 108-page
  manuscript with abstract, eight numbered sections, two appendices and a **60-item
  bibliography of real, checkable citations** (Kodaira, Mumford, Orlik, Hopf's
  original 1948 paper, and the Campana–Demailly–Peternell papers it contradicts) —
  and noted that at that moment **no Lean formalisation and no computational
  certificate accompanied it**.

**Assessment, stated as an assessment:** the strongest per-claim chain in AI
mathematics remains Anthropic's, and this is now the strongest single result in it —
a 108-page construction, an independent human reconstruction, and a compiling Lean
formalization. But *Science* says "confirmed", Wikipedia says "parts" and "scrutiny
continues", nLab says "claimed", and a Zenodo audit record is still proving missing
lemmas with CDP20 open. **The correct status is: formally verified in substantial
part, independently reconstructed, not yet universally recorded as settled.** That
divergence between a journal, an encyclopedia, a reference work and an audit record
— all within six weeks of the claim — is a concrete instance of Tao's 2026 **"proof
indigestion"** concern and of the Leiden Declaration's first threat.

**OpenAI, re-verified this turn.** *Astra* solved ten open problems with Lean
certificates published at `openai/ten-proofs` plus a **249-page** manuscript; **no
peer review yet**; *"Authorship is still being negotiated. OpenAI says it takes
responsibility for the manuscripts and Lean formalizations, while attributing the
mathematical arguments themselves to the model."* The **722-manuscript** release:
Nature reports it cost several million USD and was validated by automated
verification; on **7 October 2026 OpenAI confirmed three manuscripts withdrawn** — a
**sign error** inside one proof invalidated that result outright, and two further
manuscripts whose constructions depended on it were withdrawn with it.

**Other labs** are unchanged from X44's record and are not restated here except
where relevant: Anthropic holds the top of ProofBench outright (Opus 5.5 and Fable
5.1 at 100 %); Harmonic's Aristotle at 86 % with the verified caveat that it
**searched the web on 60 of 100 problems and solved every problem on which it did
not search**; Moonshot's Kimi K3 at 87 %; Mistral at 9–10 %; **xAI and Meta absent
from the leaderboard entirely, with no research-level mathematical result**.
**doubleAI** remains a systems lab (WarpSpeed, 3.6× over expert-written cuGraph
kernels) with no theorem.

## 8. Runtime honesty

All measurement ran **sequentially in single node processes on a 2-core sandbox**.
No parallelism, no distributed search, no independent agents, no simulators. Tools
actually used: `node`, `esbuild`, `gpt-tokenizer` (every measurement and every
encode), `tsc --noEmit` (type gate), `python3` (repo edits), `web_search` (two live
searches). **No Lean, no theorem prover, no compiler other than tsc, and no model
inference was executed.** Every mathematical and benchmark claim above is reported
from search and was not verified by me.

The eleven identical-token results and the dump.sql regression are bound to real
decodes: each row of §3 comes from a run in which the wire was decoded through the
full chain and byte-compared to the source in the same process that measured the
tokens, and `metron` was then loaded **by key** from the registry and re-verified on
two lanes.

**Not measured this turn**, and therefore not claimed: vix, aapl, openstack,
git-numstat, find-listing, npm-ls, prose-02 … prose-07 under METRON. The
twelve-lane sample is the whole basis for the cap decision, and one lane in twelve
regressed — so the honest expectation is that further lanes contain further
counterexamples.

## 9. Exact remaining gap, and the next highest-information test

**Gap:** there is no cheap predictor of when SYMPLOKE's extra 'in' shape trials pay.
Two candidates were tested and refuted (`order` does not survive the trip up a
nine-layer stack; source-level evidence was refuted in X43 and X44). Until one
exists, a static cap is a trade-off, not an improvement.

**Next highest-information test, in priority order:**
1. **A Peek2-style regex-free pretokenizer in `bpe.ts`.** arXiv 2601.05833 reports
   *identical output* to the regex baseline, and arXiv 2605.30813 puts the regex at
   **80.25 % of tiktoken/O200K CPU against 6.45 % for the merge stage**. This stack
   makes 4.24 M `countTokens` calls on a 1,797-character file and pushes 113.6 M
   characters through that regex per encode. A provably-identical pretokenizer
   would multiply through every one of them — larger, more general, and *safer* than
   any budget knob, because identical output would be a property of the
   implementation rather than a measurement over twelve lanes.
2. **A per-document cap predictor for SYMPLOKE.** Characterize dump.sql's
   uniform-width row structure against page.html's and chart.svg's markup with a
   linear scan and no tokenization, and test whether that separates the one lane
   that needs cap 4 from the eleven that do not.
3. **Correct `symploke.ts:786`.** The budget note claims "no search over the wire";
   measurement says ~1,670 full-wire tokenizations at the cheapest setting and
   ~4,240 at the default. The note should carry the measured number.
