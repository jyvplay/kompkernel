# X40 — PERITROPE (περιτροπή, “a turning round / cycle”)

Registry index **30**. `src/lib/omega/peritrope.ts`. Strict superset of ANTISTROPHE (29).

Generalises the character-exchange auction from **one** pair to a **set of pairwise-disjoint**
pairs, chosen by **beam search**.

---

ANTISTROPHE stops at one pair. Is a second pair worth anything once the first is applied?

`bench/x40/screen1.ts` (18 lanes, 15 s) applies the best pair, re-prices from scratch, and

## B. Why a set, and why disjoint

Two exchanges **collide**: swapping `␠↔/` then `/↔.` sends spaces to dots, and the reader must
know the order. PERITROPE searches over sets of **pairwise-disjoint** exchanges — no character
appears in more than one pair.

Disjointness restores what a single swap had for free: **the order of application does not
matter**. The reader gets a list, not a program.

`bench/x40/screen2.ts` (greedy, k ≤ 4, disjoint vs free):

* **identical totals on 12 of 18 lanes**
* the only lane where disjointness really binds: find-listing, −181 disjoint vs −264 free

Clause cost, measured: **≈2 tokens per pair**. Cheapest form `Then swap {a} {b}, {c} {d}.`
→ k=1 **5**, k=2 **7**, k=3 **9**, k=4 **12**. Worst form (one sentence per pair): 10/15/21.

## C. Why beam search, not greedy

The best *first* pair is not the best *first move*.

`bench/x40/screen3.ts` (WIDTH=8, DEPTH=5, CAP=10, disjoint, raw):

| lane | beam | greedy disjoint |
|---|---|---|
| find-listing | **−238** | −181 |
| pom.xml | **−49** | −33 |
| ls-full-iso | **−36** | −11 |
| npm-ls | **−229** (k=5) | — |
| readme | **−32** (k=5) | — |
| git-numstat | −421 (k=2) | −421 (tie) |
| openstack | −40 (k=2) | −36 (k=3) |

Exhaustive is out: the pool is 33 characters → 5,984 ordered triples at depth 3, each costing
one tokenization of the whole document.

Depth is chosen by `rawDelta(k) + clauseTokens(k)`, both **measured** — not by encoding every
prefix, which would cost one full stack encode per candidate depth.

## D. A bug worth recording

The pool was originally filtered by a minimum count of **20**, inherited from ANTISTROPHE. On
the same beam:

| lane | presence filter | count ≥ 20 filter |
|---|---|---|
| git-numstat | k=2 / **−421** | k=1 / −408 |
| npm-ls | k=5 / **−229** | k=1 / −11 |

A character that occurs eleven times can still be the expensive half of a very cheap pair.
The filter is now **presence + a pool-size cap** (`PERITROPE_MIN_COUNT = 1`,
`PERITROPE_POOL_CAP = 22`).

## E. Design decisions

1. **ANTISTROPHE is disabled inside the trial.** PERITROPE owns the whole permutation, so the
   exchanges it ships are disjoint **by construction** and the reader gets one clean list.
   Letting the inner ANTISTROPHE add its own pair would break that guarantee and would cost two
   extra stack encodes per candidate.
2. **The gate is measured, not assumed.** `parent = antistropheEncode(text)` — the best of
   ANTISTROPHE and OURA — and PERITROPE ships only when the full message **including the clause**
   is strictly smaller.
3. **Clause is spliced into the contract in front of `print only.`** (EUTELEIA lesson: score the
   junction, not the standalone sentence).

prime, guillemets, ¡, ¿, °, €, £, ¥, §, ¶, †, ‡, …) across 18 lanes.

**Improvement of exactly zero on all 18 lanes.** The typographic-substitution route to prose is

### G.2 npm-ls: measured as unmeasurable

npm-ls never completed. `bench/x40/npmprobe.ts` isolated why: on a **6,688-character** file,

```
ouraEncode         992.5s   tok=901
```

The **parent stack** costs 16.5 minutes on this lane, before PERITROPE runs at all. PERITROPE
multiplies that, so the lane hit 35 min and 1.6 GB RSS and was killed. This is the same
pathology that left npm-ls unmeasured in X39, and it is now localised to `ouraEncode` rather
than to anything X40 added. **npm-ls is reported as not measured, not as zero.**

## I. Honest citation — the adversarial prior art

The closest prior art for this whole family runs in the **opposite** direction.

* **SilverSpeak**, arXiv **2406.11239**: substituting 10 % of characters with homoglyphs makes
  **70 % of resulting tokens differ** in the OpenAI BPE tokenizer — a Cyrillic `а` where an `a`
  belongs has no learned merge and fragments to byte level.
* **arXiv 2508.14070** (2,800+ instances, 7 models): cross-script homoglyph substitution is the
  most effective technique at **58.7 %** guardrail bypass, 10 % (GPT-OSS 20B) to 92 % (Mistral 7B).
* **paultendo.github.io** (Feb 2026; 130 API calls, 8 attack types, 4 models, 3 document types):
  GPT-5.2 and Claude Sonnet 4.6 **correctly interpreted every substitution in every run** with
  **57 % of characters replaced** — zero meaning flips, zero clause omissions — while that same
  57 % took a 95-line contract from 881 tokens to **4,567 (5.2×)**. *“The comprehension attack
  fails. The billing attack succeeds.”*
* **arXiv 2610.03110**: homoglyph substitution evaded a RoBERTa detector in 41 % of cases;
  mapping confusables back to Latin before tokenization cut that to **2 %**.
* **arXiv 2604.10271**: ~37.5 % word-level replacement breaks authorship verification.

**How this cuts, both ways:**

* *In favour* — it is the strongest independent confirmation available that character exchange is
  transparent to a frontier reader while being extremely expensive to the tokenizer. That is the
  entire premise of X39 and X40.
* *Against* — homoglyph work substitutes **toward** rare multi-byte characters to inflate;
  PERITROPE substitutes **toward** cheap ones to deflate. Both ride the same cliff. The only
  difference is that PERITROPE never ships a direction it has not measured on the actual document,
  whereas a fixed format convention does — and McMillan's **9,649-experiment** study across 11
  models measured that class of claim reversing to **38 % MORE** tokens (χ² = 2.45, p = 0.484,
  no accuracy effect).

**Deployment caveat from the same literature:** a preprocessing layer that maps confusables back
to Latin skeletons neutralises the effect to 1.0×. Any pipeline normalising confusables at ingest
would undo this transform. Here the exchange is applied by the encoder and read by the model, not
passed through a sanitizer — but it is a real boundary, not a theoretical one.

### I.1 Tokenizer work this stack cannot use

All of these require **changing the tokenizer**, and the decoder here is whatever LLM the reader
pastes into, with its tokenizer frozen to `o200k_base`:

* **arXiv 2601.13260** — retokenization, vocabulary expansion and merge refinement are offline
  interventions that typically repay their cost with shorter sequences.
* **arXiv 2512.03989** (EACL 2026) — continued BPE training beats naive extension by up to
  **9.6 %** tokenization efficiency on average, 72.9–100 % of 70 languages improving; leaf-based
  pruning removes up to **62.5 %** (80k/128k) of vocabulary tokens without noticeable downstream
  deterioration.
* **TokEval**, arXiv **2608.18062** (COLM 2026) — evaluation metrics beyond fertility/compression.

PERITROPE is that same offline intervention achieved by rewriting the **text** instead of the
tokenizer.

## J. Greedy / non-greedy, and the bound

* **Greedy: NO.** Every candidate pair is scored on the real token count of the text it produces;
  the beam keeps **8** competing partial permutations so a locally best pair cannot lock out a
  better set; depth is chosen by measured raw delta + measured clause cost; and the transform
  ships only when the full message including the clause is **strictly** smaller than the parent.
* **Time / CPU bound: YES.** One tokenization per candidate pair per node, bounded by
  22 pool chars × 8 beam states × 10 extensions × 5 depths. Measured **165 s** find-listing,
  **305 s** git-numstat, **392 s** vix — roughly double ANTISTROPHE.
