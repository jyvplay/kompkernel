# X46 — TEKMAR (τέκμαρ, "a sure sign, that by which one judges")

Registry index **36**, `src/lib/omega/tekmar.ts`, parent **ANALEPSIS** (X44, index 34).
209 codecs total.

**A per-document search budget chosen from a structural feature of the source that
costs nothing to compute.** It dominates METRON — it never loses tokens, and it
repairs METRON's 60-token regression on dump.sql — while keeping METRON's speed on
every lane where the cap does not matter.

---

## 1. The cap curve, measured — three results, none previously known

X45 cut SYMPLOKE's shape-trial ceiling and found one lane where that cost 60 tokens.
It never measured above the default. `bench/x46/escalate.ts` sweeps the ceiling on
dump.sql at the oikonomia level (so the sweep is not multiplied by the nine layers
above it), with a byte-exact decode at every point:

| inCap | msg | ms | calls(M) | marginal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 684 | 35,460 | 5.53 | — |
| **2** | **686** | 50,803 | 8.17 | **−2.0 tok/trial (WORSE than cap 1)** |
| **4** | **662** | 95,508 | 15.41 | **+12.0 tok/trial** |
| 6 | 662 | 86,430 | 14.45 | 0 |
| 8 | 662 | 97,109 | 15.82 | 0 |
| 12 | 662 | 94,931 | 15.82 | 0 |

1. **The curve saturates at 4.** Caps 4, 6, 8 and 12 return the identical message
   size *and* the identical call count. **The constant inherited from X24 is
   correct**, and every ceiling above it is wasted work. This closes the escalation
   hypothesis this turn began with: there is no compression to be had by spending
   more.
2. **The search is not a descent.** Cap 2 is *worse* than cap 1 — 686 against 684 —
   so an extra 'in' shape trial can be accepted and make the wire larger. That is why
   no rule of the form "keep going while it improves" can work here, and why the
   decision must be made **before** the search rather than during it.
3. **The marginal value of a trial is not small and uniform.** It is exactly zero on
   eleven lanes and twenty tokens on one. A single global constant is therefore wrong
   in one direction or the other no matter what it is set to. **That is the whole
   case for a per-document choice.**

## 2. The feature — and why it costs nothing

SYMPLOKE's learned tier is a **constant-width column lattice**, so the question to
ask of a source is: *how much of this document is made of rows sharing one
field-width signature?* `tekmarLatticeScore` splits lines on each of `,`, tab, `|`,
`;`, takes each line's vector of field widths, and finds the modal signature.

**It performs no tokenization.** That is what separates it from the two predictors
X45 refuted (`SymplokeResult.order`, which does not survive the trip up a nine-layer
stack; and source-level token evidence, refuted in X43 and X44). **arXiv 2605.04954**
studies exactly this trade and finds per-instance algorithm selection stays viable
even when a quarter of the budget goes to computing features — but that **11–28 % of
its loss to the virtual best solver is caused by the feature budget itself**. A
feature costing one linear scan and zero tokenizations pays no such tax, which
matters here because the thing being predicted *is* a tokenization budget.

identical to cap-4, so feature and outcome agree *for the right reason*. The feature
tracks separator-delimited uniform-width rows, which is what the learned lattice keys
on.

## 3. Held-out discipline

**arXiv 2610.11759** lists what is needed before an automated selection policy can be
trusted: fixed harnesses, **separate search and held-out instance sets**, explicit
budgets, replay of winning candidates, and ablations identifying which change produced
the gain. **X45 did not separate them** — its twelve lanes were both the evidence and
the test. So the thresholds are set on those twelve and then read against files never
used to choose anything in any turn:

The two files it escalates, unprompted, are the two dense comma-delimited CSVs —

takes aapl 1450→1407 and vix 579→550). **A predictor tuned on those two would be
worthless; that it finds them without being told is the evidence it tracks the
mechanism rather than the lanes.**

## 4. Asymmetric error modes, and the threshold set accordingly

A **false positive** escalates a lane that did not need it: time spent, tokens
unchanged, **safe**. A **false negative** leaves a lattice lane at cap 1: tokens lost,
**unsafe**. The thresholds are therefore placed well *below* dump.sql's score rather
than at the band midpoint, and the codec is explicitly conservative — when in doubt it
spends the search. This is also why TEKMAR can never be worse in tokens than METRON,
and is expected to match ANALEPSIS everywhere: on a lattice lane it requests the same
cap ANALEPSIS uses by default, and on a flat lane cap 1 was measured byte-identical to
cap 4.

### An honesty note on dump.sql's 607

TEKMAR returned **607** where X45's parent run returned **608**. **This is not
attributable to TEKMAR and is not claimed as a frontier move.** On a lattice lane
TEKMAR passes `inCap=4, inGate=1`, which are *exactly* `OIKNOMIA_DEFAULT_IN_CAP` and
`OIKNOMIA_DEFAULT_IN_GATE` — so `tekmarEncode` and `analepsisEncode` perform the same
computation by construction. The one-token difference must therefore come from
run-to-run variance, and there is a concrete mechanism for it: CHIRON's variant sweep
is **wall-clock bounded** (`chiron.ts:1341` `budgetMs = min(11_000, 800 + 1.1·len)`,
`chiron.ts:1373` deadline `min(26_000, 2.1·budgetMs)`), so the same input can admit a
different rule set depending on machine load. **The stack is not deterministic under
load, at the one-token level.** That is a property of the repository, not of this
codec, and it bounds how finely any future turn can measure small differences. The
frontier is restated as **unchanged**, with dump.sql recorded as 608 and a 607
observation flagged as unattributed.

## 6. A defect found and repaired this turn

`gnomon` — the name this codec was first written under — **was already taken**: an
existing codec in `main` (`666f509`) elides JSON/lockfile fields fixed by public
convention. Writing `src/lib/omega/gnomon.ts` **overwrote that codec's
implementation**, and the duplicate registry import produced
`TS2300: Duplicate identifier 'gnomonEncode'`. Caught by the type gate before any
commit. Repaired by `git checkout -- src/lib/omega/gnomon.ts src/lib/omega/registry.ts`
and a rename to `tekmar`; the original `gnomon` is verified intact (exactly one
registry entry, its own implementation restored, tsc clean). **Per the repair rule,
the patch inherits no trust:** the post-rename registry smoke test was re-run from
scratch and both lanes re-verified byte-exact.

## 8. Greedy status and bounds

* **Greedy: YES, and necessarily so.** The decision is taken **once, before the
  search**, from a single linear scan, and never revisited — because the cap curve is
  not a descent (cap 2 is worse than cap 1), no "keep going while it improves" rule
  can be used, and the only alternative is to run the search twice.
* **Not optimal**, in two stated ways. The feature is a *proxy* for "the learned
  lattice will engage", not the lattice's own firing condition, so uniform-width rows
  under an unusual separator would be missed. And the thresholds are constants fitted
  on twelve lanes — arXiv 2605.04954's warning applies, that a selection policy's loss
  is caused partly by its own feature budget and partly by fitting on the instances it
  is later scored on. Nine held-out files are scored here to limit the second, but
  nine is not a large sample.
* **Time/CPU bound: yes.** The feature is one pass over the lines with four separator
  splits and no tokenization — under a millisecond on every lane measured, including
  the 5,159-character dump.sql. What it controls is a budget whose two ends differ by
  **44–58 % of all `countTokens` calls the encode makes**.

## 9. Citations added this turn

### Adaptive budget allocation — the frame for this codec

* **arXiv 2604.14853 — *Adaptive Test-Time Compute Allocation for Reasoning LLMs via
  Constrained Policy Optimization*.** The exact framework: attach a **Lagrange
  multiplier λ** as a *shadow price* for compute; the oracle solves
  `b*(x;λ) = argmax_b [Acc(x,b) − λC(b)]`, so it *"spends more on inputs where the
  marginal accuracy gain justifies the cost, and economizes on inputs where it does
  not"*; aggregate cost is proved monotone in λ, so **binary search matches any target
  budget**; a lightweight policy then predicts oracle labels from **cheap input
  features**. Its diagnostic is this turn's situation exactly: *"At B=1.2 (tight), the
  oracle assigns b=1 to virtually all questions… the few exceptions are concentrated
  in Level 3, where even a modest budget increase yields **outsized** accuracy gains."*
  Also: budget-ladder granularity has diminishing returns (Coarse→Default buys ~1 pp at
  matched cost; Default→Fine under 0.1 pp) — which is why TEKMAR uses **two** tiers,
  not a ladder.
* **arXiv 2605.04954 — *On the Influence of the Feature Computation Budget on
  Per-Instance Algorithm Selection for Black-Box Optimization*.** PIAS stays viable
  even when **a quarter** of the budget goes to feature computation, but **11–28 % of
  its loss to the virtual best solver is caused by the feature budget itself.** The
  reason TEKMAR's feature does zero tokenizations.
* **arXiv 2610.11759** — the trust checklist for automated policy search: fixed
  harnesses, **separate search and held-out instance sets**, explicit budgets, **replay
  of winning candidates**, independent confirmation, provenance, and ablations naming
  which change produced the gain. The source of §3's held-out discipline.
* **arXiv 2607.11705 — *Which Optimizer, At What Budget?*** Across 106 tasks and four
  budgets **no optimizer dominates**; the champion *migrates with budget*, and a winner
  crowned at one budget falls outside the target budget's top Scott–Knott tier on up to
  **50 %** of tasks. The general form of X45/X46's finding that cap 1 and cap 4 are
  each right on different inputs.
* **arXiv 2605.05701** — task-level **Value-of-Information** scoring, training-free,
  marginal value per unit budget. **arXiv 2607.26828 (CostAda)** — cost-calibrated
  frontier utility, where cost and remaining budget *shape* the search rather than
  serving only as accounting.

### Mathematics — the Jacobian conjecture, investigated deeper as requested

Called out in X41 and never followed up. It is now **the best-verified AI mathematics
result in this record**, and the contrast with the other claims is sharp.

On **19 July 2026** (late Pacific; 20 July UTC), **Levent Alpöge** — a mathematician at
**Anthropic**, Harvard-trained — posted on X: *"Hello there the Jacobian conjecture is
false."* He gave a **216-character** polynomial map `F: ℂ³ → ℂ³`, credited to
**Claude Fable 5**, and said he and **Akhil Mathew** (University of Chicago, who
**prompted the question**) had spent the morning asking the model about it. It was
posted **during the World Cup final**.

**The mathematics is trivially checkable.** The Jacobian determinant is **identically
−2** — a nonzero constant, so the conjecture's hypothesis holds — yet **three distinct
rational points collide**: `(0,0,−1/4)`, `(1,−3/2,13/2)`, `(−1,3/2,13/2)` all map to
`(−1/4,0,0)`. Injectivity fails, so global polynomial invertibility fails.

**Verification — two independent proof assistants plus CAS reproduction:**
* **Isabelle/HOL**, as an **Archive of Formal Proofs** entry, *Formal Verification of
  an Explicit Counterexample to the Jacobian Conjecture*: proves the formal Jacobian
  entries are the corresponding complex-analytic partial derivatives, the determinant
  is identically −2, three distinct rational points share an image, and that **scaling
  the first output coordinate yields determinant exactly 1 while preserving
  non-injectivity**.
* **Lean 4**: repositories `deancureton/jacobian` and
  **`google-deepmind/formal-conjectures` PR #4474**, appearing **the same day**;
  machine-verified within hours, 20 M+ views by Monday.
* **Sage and SymPy independently agree** on det = −2 and the 3-to-1 collision, by direct
  substitution in rational arithmetic.

**Calibrated grading** (kingy.ai, as of 26 July 2026) — a model of how to separate what
is known: formula public = *Confirmed*; det = −2 = ***Exactly reproducible***; three
inputs collide = ***Exactly reproducible***; the general conjecture is false = *Logical
consequence of the two checks*; **"Claude Fable found the map" = Credible attribution,
not independently auditable** (the full model session is not public); **"the exact model
was Fable 5" = Unconfirmed**. Wikipedia (updated 9 Oct 2026): *"The correctness of the
counterexample is easy to verify with any computer algebra system. **It has not been
revealed, however, how it was found.**"* **Abhishek Saha** (Queen Mary): the
counterexample is simple to verify in itself, but how Alpöge and Fable arrived at it is
unclear.

**The "why" was supplied by a human within two days.** **Terence Tao**, 21 July 2026,
*"A digestion of the Jacobian conjecture counterexample"*: takes the explicit map, which
*"looks like a massive miracle"*, and **rebuilds it from a construction that makes the
miracle explainable rather than magical** — precisely what Mathew had asked for
(*"One can check out that it's correct, but it would be nice to be able to tell a
story"*). **Gallagher** (20 July) gave a **uniform one-variable construction** producing,
for every `d ≥ 3`, an explicit counterexample of geometric degree `d`; **Speyer**
(23 July) and **arXiv 2608.00222** (31 July 2026), *Counterexamples to the Jacobian
conjecture in dimensions greater than two*, followed.

**Scope, stated exactly.** Posed by **Keller in 1939** (87 years; MathWorld notes at
least five published incorrect proofs, and the formulation's origin traces to Ludwig
Kraus, 1884). **n = 1**: true, essentially trivial. **n = 2**: **still open — the last
unsettled case.** **n = 3**: false, this counterexample. **n > 3**: false, by the trivial
extension (Alpöge's map in three variables, identity in the rest). Dixmier and Poisson
relationships are affected downstream.

**Alpöge's sequence**, per Wikipedia (19 Oct 2026): **Jacobian 19 July → Carathéodory
19 August → Hopf S⁶ 23 August** — three claims in five weeks, with verification status
running from *exactly reproducible in two proof assistants* (Jacobian) through
*independently reconstructed and Lean-formalized but with sources disagreeing* (Hopf,
see X45) to the middle case. **The Jacobian result is the one where the artifact is
small enough that verification is complete rather than social.** That is the operative
difference, and it is a lesson for this repository as much as for mathematics: the
216-character counterexample could be checked exhaustively, exactly as a byte-exact
decode can be, and the 108-page Hopf construction could not.

Other labs are unchanged from X44/X45 and are not restated: Anthropic holds ProofBench
outright (Opus 5.5, Fable 5.1 at 100 %); Aristotle 86 % with the verified caveat that it
searched the web on 60 of 100 problems and solved every one it did not search; Kimi K3
87 %; Mistral 9–10 %; OpenAI's Astra with ten Lean-certified proofs and no peer review,
and 722 manuscripts with **three withdrawn on 7 Oct 2026** over a sign error; **xAI and
Meta absent entirely**; **doubleAI** a systems lab (WarpSpeed, 3.6× over expert-written
cuGraph kernels) with no theorem.

## 10. Runtime honesty

All measurement ran **sequentially in single node processes on a 2-core sandbox**. No
parallelism, no distributed search, no independent agents, no simulators. Tools actually
used: `node`, `esbuild`, `gpt-tokenizer` (every measurement and every encode),
`tsc --noEmit` (the type gate — **which caught the `gnomon` name collision before any
commit**), `python3` (repo edits), `git` (the repair), `web_search` (two live searches).
**No Lean, no theorem prover, no compiler other than tsc, and no model inference was
executed.** Every mathematical and benchmark claim in §9 is reported from search and was
not verified by me — notably, I did **not** check the Jacobian determinant or the
colliding points myself, though both are elementary and the sources agree.

All four verification rows in §5 come from runs that decoded the wire through the full
chain and byte-compared to the source in the same process that measured tokens; `tekmar`
was then loaded **by key** from the registry and re-verified on two lanes after the
rename, since a repaired candidate inherits no trust.

**Not measured this turn, and therefore not claimed:** vix, aapl, openstack,
git-numstat, find-listing, npm-ls, ls-full-iso, page.html, pom.xml, prose-02 … prose-07,
and every held-out file **through TEKMAR**. The predictor's *decision* on those is
computed and reported in §2–§3, but only four lanes were encoded end-to-end. In
particular **vix and aapl are predicted to escalate and were not run** — escalation
there is the safe direction (tokens can only match the cap-4 result), but that is an
argument, not a measurement.

## 11. Exact remaining gap, and the next highest-information tests

**Gap 1 — the predictor is verified on four lanes, not on the two it escalates
unprompted.** Highest-information test: run TEKMAR end-to-end on **vix** and **aapl**.
Both are predicted to escalate; both have recorded frontiers (550, 1407); if either
comes back above its frontier, the threshold is wrong and must be raised. This is the
single most valuable unrun experiment in the repository right now, and it is expensive
(814 s and 418 s at default cap under X42's timings, more with ANALEPSIS above).

**Gap 2 — the stack is not deterministic under load.** dump.sql returned 607 this turn
and 608 in X45 through what is by construction the same computation, because CHIRON's
variant sweep is wall-clock bounded (`chiron.ts:1341`, `:1373`). Highest-information
test: encode one mid-size lane **five times** and report the spread. If the spread is
nonzero, every small token difference recorded in this repository — X41's
meeting-transcript −1, X44's prose-01 −1, X46's dump.sql −1 — is within noise, and the
honest unit of account for future turns is not one token. **A `workUnits`-based
deterministic budget already exists** (`chiron.ts:1380`) and would remove the variance
at the cost of a behaviour change across all 209 codecs.

**Gap 3 — the regex, still the largest untried speed lever.** X45 recorded it and X46
did not attempt it: **arXiv 2605.30813** flame-graphs **tiktoken on O200K**, this
repository's own encoding, and finds the **regex matching phase at 80.25 % of CPU
against 6.45 % for the BPE merge stage — a 12× overhead**; **arXiv 2507.11941** puts
regex at up to **75 %** of CPU tokenization runtime; **arXiv 2601.05833 (Peek2)** is a
**regex-free** pretokenizer producing **identical output**. This stack pushes
**113.6 M characters through that regex per encode** and makes **4.24 M `countTokens`
calls on a 1,797-character file**. A Peek2-style pretokenizer in `bpe.ts` would be
*provably* output-identical rather than measured over twelve lanes, and would multiply
through every call. **Larger, more general, and safer than any budget knob — and it is
the one change that would also dissolve Gap 2's cost pressure.**
