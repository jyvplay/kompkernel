# X37 — EUTELEIA (εὐτέλεια, "cheapness")

**Claim to be judged:** every codec in this stack injects control symbols that carry no
information from the source. They were chosen by a *collision* argument and priced as though
choosing them were free. It is not free. Repricing them, by measurement, moves the frontier.

**Result:** the frontier moves on one lane, `vix-daily-1990.csv`, from **594 → 579 tokens
(−15, −2.5%)**, byte-exact. A new codec, **EUTELEIA**, ships the mechanism. Five candidate
mechanisms were probed this turn; four are refuted with receipts and are recorded in §K.

### B1. Formatting elision — refuted (12 transforms × 20 lanes)

Whitespace run-length coding: **+1358** tokens across the corpus. Character runs: **+673**.
Line RLE +5. Indent elision, zero-padding elision, CRLF normalization, escape elision: 0.
Only span RLE pays (−759), and that is ANADIPLOSIS, which already ships.

The reason is arithmetic and it is the same arithmetic that governs §D: framing a run costs
four tokens, so a run of *k* copies of a one-token unit saves `k − 1 − 4`, and such runs are
almost never long enough. **Whitespace is cheap precisely because the tokenizer merges it into
the following word**; deleting it and framing the run costs more than the deletion saves.

### B2. Glossary serialization reorder — refuted (8 lanes, 857 s)

This was a genuine free-lunch candidate: the glossary is an explicit mapping, not a positional
one, so the order in which entries are serialized is semantically free and could be optimized
for cross-entry BPE merges (greedy nearest-neighbour plus 2-opt).

Result: **~0**. Best lane find-listing −6; vix got *worse* (+7). The mechanism fails because
the glyph characters are drawn from non-Latin scripts, and a script change is a hard
pretokenizer boundary: adjacent glossary entries **cannot** merge across it no matter how they
are ordered. There are no cross-boundary merges to harvest.

### B3. Near-repeat / delta spans — refuted (10 lanes)

Adjacent equal-length spans at Hamming distance ≤ 2 with a minimum length of 12: **zero pairs
on all ten lanes**. Not a viable mechanism.

### B4. Delimiter substitution across the whole stack — refuted (10 lanes)

The direct generalisation of §D: if the ANADIPLOSIS framing was overcharged, substitute
cheaper candidates for every other control symbol the stack injects, in particular the CHIRON
tape markers `§` (U+00A7) and `¶` (U+00B6).

Result: **0 on eight lanes, 1 token on two.** Those two characters *are* single tokens in
`o200k_base`. They were chosen well, and the collision argument happened to land on a cheap
symbol. The overcharge was **not** systemic; it was local to one symbol.

### B5. In-situ glyph repricing — refuted (3 lanes)

Each tape glyph was substituted for each of sixty unused pool glyphs and the whole wire
re-counted. The token count is **invariant**. The CHIRON claim that every pool glyph costs
exactly one token holds *in situ*, not merely in isolation, and the pool assignment is
therefore already on a plateau. (This also independently corroborates the X36 refutation of
QAP glyph reassignment.)

## C. The finding: the collision argument and the price were never compared

Every injected symbol in this stack was chosen by answering *"can this occur in real text?"*
That is a question about **safety**. The symbols were then priced at zero — or, worse, priced
at the one token they cost when tokenized **in isolation**, which is not the price they pay
when glued into a wire.

The decoder tokenizer is byte-level BPE. A code point that never occurred often enough in
training to earn a merge falls back to its raw UTF-8 bytes. **The price of a control symbol is
essentially its length in bytes.**

Measured on `o200k_base`, tokens added by the framing around one run-length span
(`bench/x37/frame.ts`, corroborated in situ by `bench/x37/fr2.ts`):

```
(N~X) 3.0   (N#X) 3.0   [N~X] 3.3   <N~X> 3.3   [N#X] 3.3
[N@X] 3.3   [N*X] 3.3   «N~X» 3.5   {N~X} 3.8   ‹N~X› 3.8
⌈N~X⌉ 6.0   ⟦N⇤X⟧ 8.8              <- the X36 choice
```

Per character, in context: `(` `)` `~` cost 1/1/1; `⟦` `⟧` `⇤` cost 3/3/2.

ANADIPLOSIS shipped `⟦ ⟧ ⇤` **because** they are vanishingly rare and can therefore never
collide with a source document. That reasoning is sound, and its conclusion is expensive:
**the rarity that buys the collision guarantee is exactly the property the tokenizer charges
for.** On `vix-daily-1990.csv`, which carries 126 run-length spans, the wire paid 8.8 tokens
of framing per span where 3.0 would do.

This is a real bug in the X36 artifact and it is now fixed. It is also the seed of the new
codec, because the fix generalizes to a *procedure*: choose injected symbols by measuring
their price, subject to the safety constraint, rather than by convention.

## D. EUTELEIA — the codec

**Registry key** `euteleia`, index 27, sits on ANADIPLOSIS. **File** `src/lib/omega/euteleia.ts`.
**Greedy: no.** **Time/CPU bound: yes.**

EUTELEIA replaces convention with measurement in two places, always against the exact
objective, and is gated on the measured full message so it can never be worse than its parent.

### D1. The framing auction

ANADIPLOSIS takes the **first** collision-free framing from a statically ordered list.
EUTELEIA tries **every** collision-free framing, tokenizes the wire each one actually
produces, and keeps the cheapest. Safety is preserved exactly — a framing is a candidate only
if none of its three characters occur in the source. What changes is that among the *safe*
choices the *cheap* one wins, measured rather than assumed.

```
ANADIPLOSIS_FRAMINGS, cheapest first, with the documented collision fallback:
  ()~   ()#   []~   <>~   []#   []@   []*   «»~   {}~   ‹›~
```

The fallback was not present in X36 and is now load-bearing: the cheap framings use ASCII
punctuation, which **does** occur in ordinary text, so a corpus containing `(` must fall
through to the next candidate. `bench/x37/rt.ts` exercises this — `page.html`,
`component.jsx`, `pom.xml` and `dump.sql` all contain `(` — and all 17 lanes round-trip
byte-exact.

### D2. The clause auction

The contract clause is spliced in front of a sentence that already exists, so its price is the
price of the **junction**, not of the clause alone. Measured (`bench/x37/cl.ts`, in situ after
two different contract tails):

```
10   (N~X) means X written N times.     <- X36
10   (N~X) means X repeated N times.
 9   (N~X)=N copies of X.
 9   (N~X): X repeated N times.
 8   (N~X)=X×N.
 7   (N~X)=X*N.                          <- chosen
 5   (N~X)XN                             <- refused: too terse to be safe
```

EUTELEIA scores every wording by measuring the **exact contract string** it produces and keeps
the minimum. `(N~X)XN` at 5 tokens is excluded from the ladder deliberately: the ladder trades
tokens for reader certainty, and a five-token clause has no verb.

### D3. Why this is a codec and not a patch

EUTELEIA is a strict superset of ANADIPLOSIS: **ANADIPLOSIS is EUTELEIA with the auctions
disabled.** The default framing and the default clause are both in play, so EUTELEIA's wire on
a document where the auctions find nothing is byte-identical to ANADIPLOSIS's. The two
auctions are new *procedures*, not new constants: they make the choice per document and score
it against the real objective, which is what the stack's other symbol choices do not do.

Encode time is not a casualty: **165 s on vix**, against roughly 200 s for the parent alone,

the winner is carried through the expensive dictionary search.

The −12 is **much smaller** than the isolated measurement predicts (5.8 tokens/span × 126
spans ≈ 731), because in the real wire the framing characters merge with the digits and
payload around them. The honest number is the measured one, not the predicted one, and the gap
between the two is itself a finding: **isolated token counts of injected symbols are not
predictive of in-situ cost.**

## G. Decodability — one chat input box

The reader receives one message: contract, then wire. For EUTELEIA the entire added
obligation is one sentence, with the framing named by the wire itself:

> (N~X)=X*N.

Expanding a span is a copy with a stated count. No arithmetic, no base conversion, no
alphabet table, no second message, no system prompt, no tools. The count is written **first**
and the unit **last**, so the parser reads a bounded digit run to the first separator and then
takes everything to the closing bracket as the unit — this ordering is load-bearing, because
the X36 writeup records the `RangeError: Invalid string length` produced by the reverse
ordering in a numeric corpus where the count's digits run into the payload's digits.

ASCII framing is, if anything, **easier** for a reader than `⟦⟧⇤` was: the characters are
ordinary, and the collision guarantee is what makes them safe.

```

    letters     tok= 144  18.3%   c/tok=3.71

```

62% of prose tokens are already word-plus-space merges at 5.37 characters per token. A
dictionary rule buys a definition to save reuse, and on prose the frequent items are already
one token, so the definition cannot be repaid. This is not a failure of the search; it is the
shape of the material.


has already taken that ground; TIMEMA's digit shapes and the dictionary own it. The remaining

## I. Frontier-lab mathematics, 2026 (individually and fractally)

No result below has any bearing on lossless coding under a frozen tokenizer. There is no
reduction from automated theorem proving to this problem, and none is claimed. The reason this
section exists is the discipline: the strongest artifacts of 2026 are short, mechanical and
hand-checkable, which is the same property this repository demands of its own contracts.

### I1. Anthropic / Claude — the Jacobian conjecture counterexample, in depth

Called out in earlier turns and now drilled into.

**The artifact.** On 19 July 2026 Levent Alpöge posted a 216-character polynomial map
F : ℂ³ → ℂ³, degree 7, with Jacobian determinant the nonzero constant **−2**, which is not
injective: the three distinct inputs (0, 0, −¼), (1, −3/2, 13/2) and (−1, 3/2, 13/2) all map to
(−¼, 0, 0). That single collision is the whole disproof. The conjecture dates to Keller, 1939,
and was on Smale's list of problems for the 21st century. It is false for all n ≥ 3; **the
two-variable case remains open** — [Wikipedia, *Jacobian conjecture*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobian_conjecture)
(updated October 2026), [New Scientist](https://www.newscientist.com/article/2580374-ais-solution-to-87-year-old-riddle-takes-mathematicians-by-surprise/).

**How far verification has actually gone.** This is where the fractal recursion lives:

| layer | status |
|---|---|
| the arithmetic | exactly reproducible; independent Sage and SymPy computations agree; checkable in any CAS in seconds |
| independent human checking | several mathematicians by the following day; Kevin Buzzard called it "a big day" |
| a geometric explanation | Terence Tao published a reconstruction of its structure, 21 July 2026 — [terrytao.wordpress.com](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/07/21/a-digestion-of-the-jacobian-conjecture-counterexample/) |
| an independent verification preprint | Zenodo 21461572, adding a cubic resolvent proving geometric degree 3, the exact image ℂ³∖M, and the identity `disc = −(54a)²·D` |
| Lean 4 formalization | Paul Lezeau (Imperial College London) formalized it by hand and opened a PR against Google DeepMind's Formal Conjectures repository, 20 July 2026, 06:16 UTC; CI passed in 1 h 39 m; **APPROVED but still not merged** as of this turn |
| a second, independent Lean 4 formalization | exists in a separate repository |
| peer-reviewed paper | **does not exist** |
| tracking | "pending peer review" at openconjectures.org/c/kill-jacobian-2026 |

**The conflict that matters and was not in earlier turns.** Alpöge is an **Anthropic
employee** — [Wikipedia, *Levent Alpöge*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levent_Alp%C3%B6ge) —
and the announcement was made on social media, not through a journal or a lab statement. The
model version is unconfirmed ("Fable 5" is the attributed name), the prompts, tools and human
steering are unpublished, and no complete transcript exists. Alpöge is also one of the two
Anthropic mathematicians who validated the zeta 67.2% result, and he announced a
counterexample to the **Carathéodory conjecture** using the same model on 19 August 2026.

**Assessment.** The mathematics is almost certainly right and the artifact is the strongest
kind of object 2026 produced: 216 characters, mechanically checkable, no machinery. But credit,
provenance and formal closure are all unresolved, and a result whose verification is a CAS call
and whose paper does not exist is not the same object as a proved theorem.

### I5. The fractal shape of 2026

The recursion is the same at every scale, and it is worth stating as a pyramid because the
ratios are the finding:

```
tier 1  claims                        722+ manuscripts, hundreds of families
tier 2  machine-consistent            ~42% claimed, 24-36% on independent recount,
                                      catalogue self-labels "unchecked"

        and actually hand-checked     counterexample
```

Claims grow superlinearly; machine-consistency grows sublinearly; independent human digestion
grows roughly linearly and in the dozens; hand-checkable closure is a set of size one.

And the recursion runs **inside** the single strongest artifact. The Jacobian map is 216
characters and reproduces in a CAS in seconds. Its Lean formalization passes CI and is approved
but not merged. Its paper does not exist. Its provenance is opaque. Its author is an employee
of the lab that receives the credit, and is also the validator of that lab's other headline
result. At every layer the object becomes slightly less closed than the layer below it
suggests.

**Relevance to this repository: none directly.** Indirectly and genuinely: the artifacts that
survive scrutiny in 2026 are the ones whose obligations can be discharged mechanically in one
pass by a reader who has been told exactly one sentence. That is the property this stack's
contracts are built to have, and it is why the clause auction in §D2 refuses the five-token
wording.

## K. Negative-space shapes

1. **Whitespace run-length coding** — eliding runs of spaces/tabs. +1358 (§B1).
2. **Character run-length coding** — eliding runs of any single character. +673 (§B1).
3. **Line run-length coding** — identical consecutive whole lines. +5.
4. **Indent elision** — leading whitespace on each line. 0.
5. **Zero-padding elision** in fixed-width numeric fields. 0 — the fixed-precision transform is
   already owned by the dictionary layer.
6. **CRLF normalization**. 0.
7. **Escape-sequence elision**. 0.
8. **Glossary serialization reorder** — the mapping is explicit so order is free. ~0; glyph
   script boundaries prevent any cross-entry merge (§B2).
9. **Adjacent near-repeat delta spans** — Hamming ≤ 2, length ≥ 12. Zero pairs exist (§B3).
10. **Delimiter substitution for `§` and `¶`** — 0 on 8 lanes, 1 on 2 (§B4).
11. **In-situ glyph reassignment across the CHIRON pool** — token count invariant (§B5).
12. **Two-marker framings** (`~NX~`, `N~X~`, `~N~X`) — all 1–2 tokens *worse* than `(N~X)` in
    situ, and every one of them makes the count ambiguous in a numeric corpus (§B of
    `fr2.ts`).
13. **Fixed-width counts** to remove the count/unit separator — the wider digit field costs
    back exactly what the removed marker saves.
14. **A five-token clause** (`(N~X)XN`) — cheapest measured, refused: no verb, so the reader's
    obligation is no longer stated.
15. **Separator stripping on the compressed wire** — worth −703 on raw aapl text and −28 on the
    wire the stack already produces. The stack owns that ground (§H).
16. **Post-grammar re-spelling** (+227), **cross-script** (tie), **CHIRON blocks** (Δ0),
    **prefix/LCP** (worse), **min-cost segmentation** (0/60), **brotli/gzip floor** (not
    LLM-executable), **digit re-spelling** (1.49 c/tok, worst), **QAP glyph assignment**
    (gain 0 under injectivity) — all carried forward from X33–X36 and not retried.

**Runtime honesty.** All timings are single measurements on a 2-CPU sandbox with no
parallelism. Encoders consult `Date.now()` internally and the `OMEGA_DET` flag does not stub
it, so the pipeline is not fully deterministic; no number in this document is an average of
repeated runs. Observed: `scan.ts` 20 lanes 0.7 s · `near.ts` 10 lanes 0.7 s · `order.ts`
8 lanes 857 s · `situ.ts` 3 lanes 52 s · `delim.ts` 10 lanes 597 s · `anat.ts` 3 lanes 1 s ·
`rt.ts` 17 lanes ≈ 40 min · `eu2.ts` vix alone 165 s. **Benches must be run alone**: two
concurrent jobs on two CPUs starve the time-based search budgets and produce spurious
`fired:false` results.

No Lean toolchain, theorem prover, or live LLM is available in this sandbox. Every claim of
decodability here is a claim about a contract's *shape*, verified by a reference decoder
written from the contract text, not by a model.
