## 1. Formal model of the open requirement

- Admissible object: a new codec `C` with encoder `E_C`, decoder `D_C`, and a one-chat message `M_C(T) = contract ‖ wire`.
- Access: the reader sees only `M_C(T)`, with no system prompt, no skills file, and no tools.
- Resource: o200k_base tokens of `M_C(T)`, contract included, against the incumbent tournament.
- Success: `∃C. (∀T ∈ lane: D_C(E_C(T)) = T byte-exact ∧ cost(C,T) ≤ cost(incumbent,T)) ∧ (∃T ∈ lane: Δ > few tokens) ∧ readable(C)`.
  `C` is fixed before the corpus; the readable half is a separate gate.
- Boundary: the readability gate is undefined until a policy is set. This is the blocking item.

## 5. Decision needed from the user (the blocking item)

The codec requirement cannot be met under the readability standard as written. The one measured candidate (TABMIN) has a gain
(−4.0% on rectangular tables, −2.2% corpus-wide) and round-trips exactly on all 169 files. Its readability gate is not passed:
table 3 does not decode exactly. Options:

1. Register TABMIN under the stack's existing standard (n ≤ 3 self-decodes, disclosed). Gain is small and niche.
2. Keep TABMIN blocked until an independent reader (not this model) decodes a pre-registered fixture set.
3. Redefine the readability gate (for example, a pass-rate threshold over several fixtures) and apply it to every codec in the stack.
