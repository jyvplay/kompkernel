# X42 — APOCOPE (ἀποκοπή, "a cutting off")

Registry index **32**, `src/lib/omega/apocope.ts`, parent **SYSTOLE** (X41, index 31).

**Headline: the gain is zero on every lane measured, and the zero is the result.**
APOCOPE is implemented, registered, byte-exact and independently verifiable. It
is shipped because a frontier needs its negative results recorded in the same
place as its positive ones — but it must not be counted as an improvement, and
on the two lanes that trigger it, it is *slower*.

What follows is the mechanism, the measurement that killed it, and the three
floors that measurement established. Those floors are the durable output of this
turn: they close off the whole numeric direction, not just this codec.

---

## 1. The mechanism

SYSTOLE (X41) established that a **separator** can be deleted because its
*position* is implied by the shape of the number. APOCOPE pushes the same
argument one step further, onto the **digits**:

> If every occurrence of a template carries the same digit at the same position,
> that digit carries no information, and can be deleted and stated once.

The motivating cases are real and common:

| lane | observation |
|---|---|
| `vix-daily-1990.csv` | all 504 prices end `0000` — a **fixed precision** |
| `vix-daily-1990.csv` | all 126 dates begin `19900` — a **fixed period** |
| `aapl-2014.csv` | all 240 dates begin `2014` |

A fixed precision and a fixed period are the same observation wearing different
clothes. Both are ubiquitous in operational data: every price in one currency
and precision, every record from one day, every build id from one release train.

### Two restrictions, both for the reader

**Only a prefix and a suffix — never an interior position.** This is a
reader-capacity limit, not an encoder convenience. `prepend 2014` and
`append 0000` are single local edits. Deleting an *interior* digit forces the
reader to interleave two digit strings by counting into both — exactly the
procedural task that [arXiv 2605.02028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02028) shows
models fail at, across **126 model variants**, every one with a finite counting
capacity far below its nominal context.

**Only when 100 % constant.** A single exception turns the affix into an
exception list, and an exception list is a *second* rule the reader must apply
conditionally. That is where silent corruption starts. One deviation and the
affix stays.

### Relation to a mechanism already recorded as dead

"Fixed-precision trailing zeros" is in this repository's dead list. It is dead
**without the template framing**: a global trailing-zero strip is not invertible,
because the reader cannot know how many zeros to put back. Inside a template the
width is fixed, so the count of restored zeros is determined. APOCOPE is the
version of that idea that *is* invertible — which is why it was worth building
even though it did not pay.

### Composition

APOCOPE **wraps** SYSTOLE rather than competing with it. Separators are removed
first, then the affix is cut from the resulting digit string; decoding restores
the affix first and the separators second. The reader identifies a run by the
width that survives *both* steps, so SYSTOLE's one-template-per-width rule is
inherited and enforced on the surviving width.

### The clause, priced before it was chosen

`bench/x42/clause.ts` priced six wordings on both lanes. The shipped form
renders each template in full, constants literal and survivors as `#`:

```
Restore 4:2014-##-##, 9:1##.#######.
Restore 3:1990-0#-##, 4:##.##0000.
```

Measured cost against the SYSTOLE clause it replaces: **+1 token on aapl, +3 on
vix**. A bare form without the surviving width is ~4 tokens cheaper and was
rejected, because the width is the key the reader matches runs against and
dropping it converts a lookup into a count
([CharBench, arXiv 2508.02591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02591)).

## 3. Why: three floors

`bench/x42/screen10.ts` is the measurement that explains the zero, and it is the
most informative single result of the turn.

### The cut text is HALF the size raw — and compresses to the same thing

| lane | source raw | cut raw | source → stack | cut → stack |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| vix | 3412 | **1774** (−48 %) | 550 | ~530–550 (rejected) |
| aapl | 3108 | **2062** (−34 %) | 1407 | ≥1387 (rejected) |

Removing **1,638 raw tokens** from vix moved the compressed size by **zero**.
The stack's output is pinned to the *information content* of the data, not to
its raw size. That is what a good compressor does, and it is the single
strongest piece of evidence in this repository that the stack is at its ceiling.

The screen's own prediction (756 for vix) under-measured by more than 2×,
because it priced digits in isolation and missed the boundary effects. The full
raw delta is the honest number.

### Floor 1 — `ceil(W/3)` is a hard floor for digit runs. Proven exhaustively.

`bench/x42/digittok.ts` enumerated **all 1,110,000 digit strings of length 1–6**:

| length | count | single-token | 
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10 | **100 %** |
| 2 | 100 | **100 %** |
| 3 | 1,000 | **100 %** |
| 4 | 10,000 | **0.00 %** |
| 5 | 100,000 | **0.00 %** |
| 6 | 1,000,000 | **0.00 %** |

`1990`, `2024`, `2026`, `1000`, `0000`, `1024`, `4096` are all **2 tokens**.
Inserting any of `.` `,` `-` ` ` or nothing at any interior position of a real
9- or 10-digit price **never beat the bare run** (774453947: bare 3, best 3;
17240000: bare 3, best 3; 20140102: bare 3, best 3; 1009479319: bare 4, best 4).

On the shipped aapl message the 364 digit runs hold 2,300 digits and cost
**823 tokens**; `sum(ceil(W/3))` over those same runs is **also 823**.
**Headroom is exactly zero** — the digits are not merely good, they are optimal.

> Reducing the token count of a digit run therefore requires reducing the
> **number of digits**, and there is no other route.

### Floor 2 — the non-digit layer is also at its floor

`bench/x42/glyph.ts` on the same aapl payload:

- 786 non-digit characters, **76 distinct**, 535 tokens
- per-character entropy **5.89 bits** → **8.66 bits per token**
- total entropy 4,632 bits = 579 bytes

8.66 bits/token is *above* the 8-bit byte ceiling and a factor of ~2 below the
`log2(200000) = 17.6`-bit absolute limit for a vocabulary that size. At the
usual 6-bits/token rule of thumb the same stream would cost 772 tokens instead
of 535 — i.e. the current encoding is **better than the rule of thumb**.

Reassigning the glyph alphabet to raise bits/token is blocked by a general
argument, not by measurement: the decoder is a bare LLM in one chat box with no
system prompt, so any alphabet mapping must be **stated in the message**. For a
76-character alphabet that costs more than the ~142 tokens it could save. This
independently explains why "in-situ CHIRON glyph reassignment" is in the dead
list.

### Floor 3 — the dictionary already hoists constants

APOCOPE deletes redundancy. The stack's dictionary **is** a redundancy engine.
They collide head-on, and the dictionary wins on its own ground. It is visible
in the wire: the aapl message opens

```
201412Отөк8ёсөү1әб5Асър4Ѕсп412¶AAPL_x,AAPL_y...
```

with the year `2014` emitted **exactly once** and every later date reduced to a
short run. The transform APOCOPE proposes has already been performed by a
different mechanism before APOCOPE ever sees the text.

### The general lesson

This refines what X41 recorded:

> **What survives the stack is tokenization-*boundary* change, not redundancy
> removal.**

SYSTOLE and PERITROPE pay because removing a separator or swapping a character
changes *where the tokenizer can cut* — something no dictionary can do. APOCOPE
does not pay because deleting a repeated digit only removes redundancy, which is
precisely what the dictionary already eats for free.

### Independent reference decode of the clause

The mechanism never ships, but it must still satisfy the contract every codec in
this repository meets: **a bare reader, given only the clause text, can restore
the data.** `bench/x42/handcheck.py` is an independently written Python decoder
that parses `Restore 3:1990-0#-##, 4:##.##0000.` with a regex and rebuilds the
vix head from the cut text. It does **not** import `apocope.ts`.

```
cut      : 102,1724,1724,1724,1724
restored : 1990-01-02,17.240000,17.240000,17.240000,17.240000
md5(source) == md5(restored) == 10481361b3b879c04d1f3037de9dfcb3
```

Artifacts: `bench/x42/vix-SOURCE-head.txt`, `vix-CUT-head.txt`,
`vix-RESTORED-head.txt`, `hand.ts`. This is a receipt that the layer is **sound
and literally readable**, not a receipt for a win.

## 4. Two further mechanisms refuted this turn

`bench/x42/diag.ts` measured both across all 17 lanes before either was built.

**Case folding is dead.** Capitalised word-initials were priced against their
lowercase forms. **7 of 17 lanes are negative** (capitalised forms often
tokenize *cheaper*): bibliography −20, page.html −15, email-thread −10,
meeting-transcript −8, kb-article −4, openstack −3, component.jsx −2. The best
positive is dump.sql at +29 on 13 cased words, which does not survive a clause.

**Phrase-level n-gram dictionaries are exhausted on prose.** Every prose lane
has a **saturation ratio above 1**, meaning the stack already removes *more*
than the entire phrase-repeat budget:

| lane | saved | word+phrase budget | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| bibliography | 229 | 52 | **4.40** |
| git-numstat | 2726 | 1151 | 2.37 |
| ls-full-iso | 809 | 400 | 2.02 |
| pom.xml | 302 | 163 | 1.85 |
| prose-01 | 48 | 29 | 1.66 |
| component.jsx | 32 | 20 | 1.60 |
| meeting-transcript | 88 | 55 | 1.60 |
| email-thread | 141 | 100 | 1.41 |
| readme | 197 | 182 | 1.08 |

Phrase repeats *are* much larger than word repeats (page.html: 105 word vs
**3,384** phrase-4), which is why this was worth measuring — but a budget
already overspent cannot be harvested twice.

### The prose gap, closed with numbers

Twelve fresh prose holdouts in `bench/x30/fresh`, **ten never used in any prior
turn**, were encoded through the full stack (`bench/x42/prose.ts`):

| lane | src | msg | instr | payload | c/tok |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| prose-01 | 785 | 737 | 11 | 726 | **4.12** |
| prose-02 | 879 | 789 | 19 | 770 | 3.17 |
| prose-03 | 1003 | 837 | 11 | 826 | 3.53 |
| prose-04 | 877 | 836 | 11 | 825 | 3.50 |
| prose-05 | 1070 | 920 | 11 | 909 | 3.16 |
| prose-06 | 926 | 840 | 11 | 829 | 3.22 |
| prose-07 | 861 | 809 | 16 | 793 | 3.86 |

All EXACT. Raw English sits near 3.8–4.5 chars/token, so these payloads are at
or *above* natural English density — and on prose-02 the compressed stream is
**3.17 chars/token against the source's own density**, i.e. the stack is
measurably worse per character than the untouched text on that lane.

Instruction overhead is **11 tokens** on every clean prose lane (1.4 % of the
message). There is no overhead to reclaim.

`bench/x42/screen9.ts` additionally checked the untested gap that prose-02..12
had **never** been passed through SYSTOLE. Their structured-numeric raw savings
are 0–27 tokens, against a SYSTOLE clause that costs 15–19 — below threshold,
and the ~20× attenuation through the stack makes them worthless. Prose is now
confirmed saturated by a **fourth** independent route.

## 5. Greedy status, citation honesty, bounds

**Greedy:** *no* for affix selection — each candidate is priced on the real token
count of the text it produces, and every surviving set is re-verified for exact
invertibility and reader-unambiguity; selection runs to a **fixed point**
because ambiguity is not monotone in the template set (inherited from X41).
*No* for the parent gate — the parent is encoded and the worse of the two
discarded. Greedy in exactly one respect: templates are considered in order of
measured saving.

**Time and CPU bound:** *yes* for detection — one regex pass plus a
longest-common-prefix and longest-common-suffix over the occurrences, both
linear. The gate costs a third full stack encode where the mechanism looks
viable (814 s vix, 418 s aapl), and nothing where it cannot pay.

**Honest size:** **zero tokens on every lane measured.**

### Citations that cut against this codec

- [arXiv 2402.14903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14903) (Singh & Strouse) and the
  2026 dev.to/ji_ai analysis both argue for **adding** structure to numbers so a
  model can compute with them. APOCOPE removes digits outright, which is a
  strictly larger step in the direction they measure as harmful. A wire from this
  codec family is a worse substrate for downstream arithmetic, and the token
  count does not see that cost.
- [arXiv 2602.17004](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17004) §2.1.1 (Arcee Trinity
  Large) documents catastrophic backtracking in digit-run regexes. APOCOPE
  reuses SYSTOLE's trimmed-and-resumed scanner and never a nested lookahead.
- [arXiv 2601.14658](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14658): tokenizers admit
  **non-unique encodings**, a standing caution for any accounting done in tokens.

### Newly cited, on this codec's own substrate

- [arXiv 2604.13066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13066) — *Lossless Prompt
  Compression via Dictionary-Encoding and In-Context Learning*. The closest
  published analogue of this entire stack: frequent subsequences become
  meta-tokens and the model is instructed to substitute them back, with a
  token-savings condition `(1+f)·n_token(M) + n_token(S)` governing acceptance.
  Notably it relies on a **system prompt** for the dictionary, which this
  repository's one-chat-box constraint forbids.
- [arXiv 2605.29000](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29000) — strategic deletion with
  LLM reconstruction. At high retention a **regular** deletion pattern preserves
  distributed context better than random deletion; under heavy compression a
  Gaussian schedule overtakes it.
- [arXiv 2609.35475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35475) — *Spontaneous Context
  Restoration*. In controlled attention-only transformers the ability to recover
  from deleted or corrupted tokens **emerges despite training exclusively on
  clean sequences**, with no denoising objective. This is the closest thing to a
  theoretical licence for the restore-by-rule family SYSTOLE and APOCOPE belong
  to.
- [arXiv 2604.13006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13006) — a single instruction,
  *"don't use commas"*, shrinks model responses by up to **48 %**; GPT-4o-mini
  falls 472→216 words and loses 31 % of coverage. A formatting constraint is
  never free even when it is cheap to state.
- [arXiv 2605.00817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00817) — accuracy 63 % at 5
  procedural steps falling to 20 % at 95, which is why APOCOPE refuses interior
  deletions.

## 6. Mathematics review — new sources this turn

### Two institutional declarations, not previously cited here

**The Leiden Declaration on Artificial Intelligence and Mathematics**, published
**2 June 2026** by 16 mathematicians from 15 universities after a September 2025
Lorentz Center workshop; endorsed by the **International Mathematical Union**;
[arXiv 2609.35334](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35334); August 2026 *Notices of
the AMS*; **23 recommendations** at four levels (individuals, organisations and
funders, policymakers, commercial labs). It passed **1,854 signatures within a
week** and roughly 2,000 by mid-June, with Fields Medalists **Terence Tao, Peter
Scholze, Elon Lindenstrauss, Martin Hairer, Hugo Duminil-Copin, James Maynard
and Maryna Viazovska** among the signatories. It names five threats, the first
being that automated techniques "can produce plausible but unreliable (or even
incorrect) arguments which are difficult to distinguish from correct
mathematical proofs."

**"A Severe Misalignment of AI in Mathematics"**, published **11 September 2026**
at mathandai.org, signed by **25 Fields Medalists** — Tao, Scholze, Viazovska,
Caucher Birkar, June Huh, Martin Hairer, Maxim Kontsevich, James Maynard, Manjul
Bhargava, and 2026 medallist **Yu Deng** — spanning medal classes from 1978 to
2026. It passed **7,200 signatories by 16 September**.

Scholze's endorsement of the earlier declaration carries a personal statement
worth quoting: *"I am pondering my mathematical ideas without use of AI, and
generally avoid reading AI-generated text as best as I can."*

Read against X41's per-lab record, these are a direct institutional verdict. The
Leiden threat list maps one-to-one onto OpenAI's 6 October 2026 release of 722
manuscripts in 372 result families from an unreleased model — three withdrawn
within a day over a **sign error**, ~58 % resting on unchecked natural language.
The disclosure the declarations demand is exactly what that release omitted:
average compute published, **no prompts, no model**.

### Labs not covered before, evaluated individually

- **DeepSeek** — different in kind from the others. **DeepSeekMath-V2**, released
  27 November 2025 ([arXiv 2511.22570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22570)), is a
  **685-billion-parameter** model built **verifier-first**: a trained verifier
  emits a natural-language analysis plus a score in {0, 0.5, 1}, with reward
  weights **α = 0.76** on the proof score and **β = 0.24** on the self-analysis;
  a **meta-verifier** then reviews the verifier's own analysis. Reported
  gold-level on IMO 2025 and CMO 2024, **118/120 on Putnam 2024**, ~99 % on
  IMO-ProofBench Basic though behind Gemini Deep Think on the Advanced subset.
  The meta-verifier is the load-bearing detail: without it a learned verifier can
  score a proof correctly while **hallucinating the issues it claims to find**;
  DeepSeek report the mean quality score of verifier analyses rising **0.85 →
  0.96** with score-prediction accuracy held. This is a verification
  *architecture*, not a solved open problem — but it attacks precisely the
  unreliability both declarations warn about, and it is the most transferable
  idea here: **this repository's `handcheck.py` is a meta-verifier**, an
  independently written decoder that reviews the encoder's claim rather than
  trusting it.
- **xAI** — **no research-level mathematical result found.** Grok 4.7 shipped
  21 September 2026 (500K context) with benchmark scores only; Grok 4.20's
  four-agent cross-verification design (coordinator / research / logic-math /
  contrarian) is an architecture claim, not a theorem. The absence is reported
  rather than filled in.
- **Meta** — **no research-level mathematical result found.** Muse Spark 1.2
  ships vendor-run benchmark numbers (82.9 % Terminal-Bench 2.1) that are
  explicitly not independently verified.
- **Google DeepMind** — unchanged from X41 and still the only lab with a
  *completed* review: AlphaProof Nexus, 9 of 353 open Erdős problems (two open
  56 years), peer reviewed in **Science**, October 2026.

The calibration number still stands: of 700 open Erdős problems surveyed in
January 2026, **13 were addressed, 5 seemingly new, 8 already in the
literature.**

## 7. Runtime honesty

Everything ran sequentially in single node processes on a 2-core sandbox —
**no parallelism, no distributed search, no independent agents.**

Tools actually used: `node` + `esbuild` + `gpt-tokenizer` (all measurement and
all encodes), `tsc --noEmit` (type gate), `python3` (repo edits), and
`web_search` (4 live searches). **No Lean, no theorem prover, no model inference
was executed.** All mathematical claims above are reported from search, not
verified by me.

The exhaustive digit enumeration in §3 is a genuine exhaustive enumeration —
1,110,000 strings tokenized individually, not a sample.

### What is left, honestly

Three floors now stand between this stack and further gains, and each is
measured rather than argued:

1. **Digits are at `ceil(W/3)`, exhaustively proven, headroom exactly 0.**
2. **The glyph layer runs at 8.66 bits/token, above the byte ceiling**; raising
   it requires stating an alphabet mapping in-message, which costs more than it
   saves.
3. **Redundancy removal is fully captured by the dictionary** — demonstrated by
   deleting 1,638 raw tokens from vix and moving the compressed size by zero.

The only directions these do not close are ones that change *what the reader has
to do* rather than what the text says — and those trade token count against
comprehension, which is the bound CharBench measures and the bound this
repository has been living under since X33.
