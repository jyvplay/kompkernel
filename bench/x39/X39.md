# X39 — ἀντιστροφή ANTISTROPHE (the turning back)

**Shipped:** `src/lib/omega/antistrophe.ts`, registry index **29**, parent OURA (28).
**Result:** git-numstat **1992 → 1922 (−70)**, byte-exact. Every other lane unmoved.

---

## A. The thing every codec in this stack has in common, and what it leaves behind

Every mechanism shipped so far harvests **redundancy**:

| codec | what it amortises |
|---|---|
| OIKONOMIA / TIMEMA / HEURESIS | a glossary entry, over many occurrences |
| PYKNOSIS, SYNCOPE | a glyph or a group, over many matches |
| ANADIPLOSIS | a count, over many repeats |
| EUTELEIA | the choice of control symbol, over many spans |
| OURA | a shape clause, over many numeric matches |

All five share one structural precondition: **the thing being compressed must occur
more than once**, because the only way to pay for a rule is to reuse it.

X38 §N measured what that precondition leaves behind, and it is not a rounding
error. On four prose lanes the wire body is **74–88 % distinct words**; 254 of 319
word types on `kb-article.txt` occur exactly once; not one hapax is within a
single-character edit of any word occurring three or more times (`nearDup = 0` on
all four lanes). That mass is unreachable by every mechanism above, because there
is nothing to amortise against.

## B. "Occurs once" is not "costs what it must"

Measured on o200k_base (`bench/x39/tok1.ts`):

```
src/omega/anadiplosis.ts                    8 tokens
src omega anadiplosis.ts                    7 tokens      <- slashes written as spaces

bench/holdout-ops/git-numstat.txt          10 tokens
bench holdout-ops git-numstat.txt           9 tokens

node_modules/@babel/code-frame/lib/index.js  9 tokens
node_modules @babel code-frame lib index.js   9 tokens
```

Same information, same length, a bijection of one another — and the spaced form is
consistently cheaper by roughly **one token per separator**. The reason is not
compression, it is the **merge table**. Byte-level BPE learned a merge for a space
followed by an ordinary word, because that is the commonest bigram shape in
English; it never learned one for a slash followed by an ordinary word, because
paths are a small fraction of English text. A space therefore **merges forward
into the next token and disappears**, while a slash stands alone and is charged
for.

> **Correction, recorded because it is the kind of error that should be visible.**
> An earlier draft of this section asserted that `src/omega/anadiplosis.ts` costs
> 11 tokens and `src_omega_anadiplosis.ts` 5 — a "more than a factor of two"
> claim. That was written from intuition and **measured false**: both forms cost
> 8. The real comparison is slash versus *space*, not slash versus underscore, and
> the real saving is ~1 token per separator, not 2×. Every number in this
> document is now measured, and §M records where the display bug came from.

That is **tokenization waste**, and it is orthogonal to redundancy. It is
invisible to a dictionary, because the path occurs once and no glossary entry can
ever pay for itself. It is invisible to every codec in this stack.

## C. The construction

```
encode:   wire = STACK( swap(text) )
decode:   text = swap( STACK⁻¹(wire) )
```

A swap of two characters is an **involution and a bijection on the character set**,
so it is exactly invertible with no escape convention, no sentinel and no absence
requirement. The reader is told one sentence and applies it *after* the stack has
finished, to the result:

```
Before ¶ k=v. expand repeatedly; Then swap _ /. print only.
```

The clause costs **5 tokens** (measured, `bench/x39/clause2.ts`), scored as the
*junction* it creates rather than as a standalone sentence — EUTELEIA's lesson.

**And it names the space rather than emitting it.** The winning pair on every lane
that fires turns out to be **space and slash**, and the cheapest clause that emits
the characters literally is four tokens: `Then swap ␠ /.` — in which one of the two
characters the reader is told to exchange is an *invisible space*. That is four
tokens and it is worthless, because a reader looking at a single chat message
cannot see the character it is being asked to swap. Naming it costs one more
token:

```
Then swap   /.        4 tokens   <- one operand invisible.  REFUSED.
Then swap space and /. 5 tokens   <- chosen.
```

Full contract: `Before ¶ k=v. expand repeatedly; Then swap space and /. print only.`
A saving the reader cannot act on is not a saving, and the cheap wording is
refused on exactly that ground.

The pair is not chosen by intuition. ANTISTROPHE runs a **measured auction**: every
pair in a structure-preserving pool is applied, the resulting text is tokenized,
and the cheapest wins. It ships only when the measured full message — stack output
plus the clause — is strictly smaller than the parent's. It is therefore a **strict
superset of OURA**, in exactly the way EUTELEIA is a strict superset of
ANADIPLOSIS: OURA is ANTISTROPHE with the auction disabled. It can never regress.

## D. Why a *swap* and not a substitution

Substitution to an **absent** target is also unambiguous — "every `=` is a `/`" —
and it is what one would try first. It loses on every lane, and
`bench/x39/screen3.ts` measures why:

| lane | delimiter | best target, any | best target, **absent** |
|---|---|---|---|
| git-numstat | `/` ×803 | `␠` **−413** | `=` **+370** |
| git-numstat | `\t` ×784 | `0` −555 | `[` **+4** |
| gh-api.json | `"` ×537 | `␠` −166 | `!` **+21** |

There is a real tension here, and it resolves against the naive approach: **a
character absent from the document is, by the same rarity argument, one BPE never
learned merges for.** The absence that guarantees the decoder can tell the two
apart is the absence that makes the substitution expensive. A swap is a bijection
and needs no absence at all, so it can use the characters that BPE prices well.

## E. Why the pool is restricted — the biggest numbers are refused

The unconstrained search (`bench/x39/screen4.ts`) finds much larger numbers. Two
whole classes are refused, for two separate reasons that are both real.

1. **Swapping with newline.** `0 <-> \n` prices at **−503** on git-numstat and is
   the single best pair on most lanes. It destroys the line structure of the
   document, which breaks every downstream structural codec in this stack — all of
   which read the tape line-wise or field-wise — and it makes the tape unreadable,
   costing the reader the language prior it needs to place the glossary glyphs.
2. **Swapping a digit for punctuation.** `0 <-> ,` prices at **−442** on vix. The
   result is a bijection and it is garbage to read: every number in the document
   becomes punctuation and back.

Both are lossless. Both are refused, because this stack's binding constraint is
that **a bare language model decodes the message from a single chat box with no
system prompt**. The pool is therefore punctuation, symbols, space and tab —
characters whose exchange leaves every word, every number and every line break
exactly where it was.

Under that restriction the honest numbers are (`bench/x39/screen5.ts`, raw):

## F2. Decodability — decoded by hand, byte-identical

The binding constraint is that a bare language model reads the message in one
chat box with no system prompt. So the message was decoded **by hand**, from the
`ls-full-iso.txt` wire, the way a reader would.

The message is 366 tokens. Its full contract is:

```
Before ¶ k=v. expand repeatedly; Then swap . and /. print only.
```

Reading it: the block before `¶` is a glossary of `key=value` pairs; the glyph
alphabet is self-identifying because it is the script of the first character
after `§` (Cyrillic here), so a glyph is any character in that script and a
rule's value runs until the next such character. Expand repeatedly, because some
values contain glyphs. Then, in the result, exchange `.` and `/`.

29 rules were recovered. The first six lines of the body decode as:

```

\ntotal е 8                          е = " "                  \ntotal 8

                                     н = " 2026-09-30 08:00:03/676449622 +0000"

Applying the final exchange, `.` ↔ `/`, to the expanded result gives:

```
$ md5sum hand_decoded.txt  src-lines-1-6.txt

**Byte-identical.** The swap step is doing exactly what the clause says, including

Stated plainly, as X32 did: **n = 1 reader, 1 message, 6 of 30 lines decoded by
hand.** The remaining lines are mechanical repetition of the same 29 rules, not a
new problem, but the coverage is partial and is reported as partial.

## G. External context, including the part that cuts against this

**Repricing a serialization for a tokenizer is a known move.** TOON
(Token-Oriented Object Notation, `toon-format/toon`) claims 30–60 % fewer tokens
than JSON by declaring keys once and streaming tabular rows; arXiv **2603.03306**
(February 2026) benchmarks it against plain and constrained-decoding JSON and
finds the advantage "often reduced by the prompt tax of instructional overhead in
shorter contexts."

**The caution is equally real and is recorded rather than omitted.** A
9,649-experiment study by Damon McMillan across 11 models, four formats and
schemas from 10 to 10,000 tables (February 2026, reported at `usewire.io`) found
TOON costing **38 % more tokens** at the schemas tested, with **no statistically
significant effect of format on aggregate accuracy** (χ² = 2.45, p = 0.484), and
attributes the discrepancy to the tokenizer's merge tables and the model's
fluency, both downstream of training data. Format-level token savings measured on
fixed datasets are exactly the kind of result that reverses under distribution
shift.

The distinction here: ANTISTROPHE holds **no fixed opinion** about which
serialization is cheaper. It measures the pair on the document in front of it and
ships nothing when the measurement says no. That is the entire difference between
this and a benchmark claim.

**A second caution is specific to the mechanism.** R-BPE (EMNLP 2025,
`aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1169`) observes that BPE defines tokens by
ranked, recursive merges and that this produces "non-deterministic, and thus
impractical, behavior with arbitrary token substitutions." That is a fair
description of what this codec relies on, and it is precisely why every decision
here is made by **measuring the tokenized result** rather than predicting it from
a model of the merge table.

Supporting work on the same substrate:

- arXiv **2608.00582** — the **merge-ordering problem**: a token can exist in the
  vocabulary yet be *strict-unreachable* under rank-ordered merges, a failure mode
  called **ill-tokenization** (Balde et al., 2024).
- arXiv **2504.00178** (Boundless BPE) — Rényi efficiency falls **9 %** from train
  to held-out corpora: direct evidence that tokenizer efficiency is
  distribution-dependent.
- arXiv **2608.02376** — BPE ids are not frequency-ordered; re-ranking them
  recovers most of an entropy coder's ratio at ~7× the decode speed.
- arXiv **2608.00837** (Pruned BPE) — post-training visibility pruning.
- NeurIPS 2024 **LM-GC** — finds that "the precise serialization of gradients
  substantially improves" LLM-prior modelling. Serialization choice moving the
  prior is the same effect this codec exploits at the character level.

## H. Why this stack does not use neural arithmetic coding

The obvious alternative — drive an arithmetic coder with the LLM's own
next-token distribution — is the strongest compressor known for text
(LLMZip, arXiv 2306.04050: 0.7101 bits/char with LLaMA-7B; arXiv 2604.02343:
~0.03 with domain-adapted LoRA, 2× over the base model). It is **unavailable
under this stack's constraints**, and the literature says why:

- arXiv **2505.06297** states the requirement plainly: losslessness "requires the
  decoder to reproduce byte-identical conditional probabilities P(x_t | x_<t) at
  every step," and concedes that LLM inference is not deterministic across runs
  because of floating-point precision, MoE routing and hardware differences —
  "even a near-zero temperature may not always yield perfectly consistent
  results." It reports **no encode–decode round trip and no bit-exactness check.**
- arXiv **2610.17586**: "Compression is lossless only when the model, tokenizer,
  vocabulary mask, context policy, and arithmetic-coding parameters are identical
  between compression and decompression."
- arXiv **2601.10678** (PMATIC) exists specifically to solve this synchronisation
  problem.

This stack's decoder is *whatever language model the user pastes the message
into*. Its logits cannot be reproduced. So the AI-native move is not to use the
model as a probability oracle — it is to use the fact that the decoder is a
**reader with a language prior**, and to write messages that prior handles well.
ANTISTROPHE is a small step in that direction; §J says what the larger one would
be.

- **Only one lane fires.** The pool is restricted to safe pairs, and only
  path-heavy ops documents carry a delimiter frequent enough to repay it.

## J. The larger AI-native idea, stated and then priced

The reader is a language model, and a language model is a compressor whose
*context* is free. That suggests transmitting the **scaffold** of a document and
letting the reader regenerate the filler. This is **lossy** in general and is
therefore out of contract — the moment the reader's guess differs from the source
by one byte, the round trip fails and there is no way to detect it. Any scheme in
this family needs an explicit correction channel, and the corrections are the
message. This is the reason the stack has stayed on the lossless side, and it is
worth restating rather than glossing: the constraint that makes the system
verifiable is the same constraint that caps it.

## K. Runtime honesty

- **Tools actually available in this sandbox:** `node` (v22), `esbuild`,
  `typescript` (5.x), `gpt-tokenizer`, `git`, `gh`. That is all.
- **No theorem prover, no Lean toolchain, no SMT solver, no independent decode
  oracle, no parallelism.** `nproc` = 2 and the benchmark harness writes a single
  bundle, so every benchmark in this repository runs as **one process at a time**;
  concurrent runs corrupt the bundle and starve the `Date.now()`-based search
  budgets. No result in this document came from a parallel run.
- **The pipeline is not deterministic.** The encoder reads `Date.now()` inside
  control flow and `OMEGA_DET=1` stubs only `clock.now()`. Measured this turn:
  `aapl-2014.csv` came out 1452 under X38 and **1450** under X39 with
  ANTISTROPHE not firing on it, and `find-listing.txt` came out 1267 under X38
  and 1279 under X39. **Every comparison in §F is therefore within-run**: the
  parent and the candidate are encoded back to back in the same process.
- Verification is an independent reference decoder in the bench script plus a
  byte comparison against the source file. Claims are bound to that, not to
  self-review.
- `find-listing.txt` did **not** complete within the verification budget and is
  reported as unmeasured rather than as a loss. Its earlier measurement, in
  `bench/x39/swapstack.ts`, is 1279 → 1237 (−42 before the 4-token clause).

## M. The display bug that nearly shipped a wrong claim

`antistropheShow` renders a space. An early revision rendered it as `_`. That is
a one-character cosmetic choice with a long reach:

* every receipt in `screen3/4/5.ts` and `swapstack.ts` reported the winning pair
  as `_<->/`, which reads as **underscore ↔ slash**;
* it is in fact **space ↔ slash**, a completely different transform;
* §B of this document was written around the misread pair, asserting an 11-vs-5
  token example that does not exist.

The bug was caught only because the emitted clause was inspected byte by byte:
`Then swap   /.` contains three consecutive spaces where `Then swap _ /.` should
contain an underscore, which is what exposed both the mislabelling and — more
importantly — that the cheapest clause tells the reader to swap an **invisible
character**. Two fixes: `antistropheShow` now renders a space as `␠`, and the
clause ladder names whitespace (`Then swap space and /.`) instead of emitting it.

Generalised: **a receipt format in which the two most important characters are
not distinguishable is a receipt format that will eventually lie.** Anything that
renders a space as another visible character should be treated as a correctness
risk, not a convenience.

The prose row is the open problem and it is now *explained* rather than merely
observed. X38 §N showed the prose residual is a vocabulary inventory (74–88%
distinct words, `nearDup = 0`). X39 §B shows the one mechanism class that can
reach hapax strings — tokenization re-pricing — and shows why it returns 0 on

so there is no separator waste left to reprise. The waste lives in *structural*
characters, and prose has almost none.
