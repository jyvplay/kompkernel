# X39 — ἀπογραφή APOGRAPHE (the register)

*Every codec in this stack is a substitution codec. This one is not, and that is the
whole point.*

Measured this turn, byte-exact, with the parent and the child encoded in the same
process, `OMEGA_DET=1`, `o200k_base`, one chat message, no system prompt, no skills file,
no tools, no second message:

| lane | frontier parent M | **APOGRAPHE M** | Δ | % | exact | header written | modelled / literal rows | encode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `aapl-2014.csv` (5 488 B) | 1 450 | **1 151** | **−299** | −20.6 % | yes | `<2|,|1=workdays>` | 232 / 9 | 295 s |
| `vix-daily-1990.csv` (6 578 B) | 579 | **409** | **−170** | −29.4 % | yes | `<5|,|1=workdays|3=col2|4=col2|5=col2>` | 122 / 5 | 267 s |
| `psql-output.txt` (1 578 B) | 322 | **289** | **−33** | −10.2 % | yes | `<7;|;2=+1;3=+1>` | 23 / 2 | 22 s |

M is the o200k token count of the whole one-chat message (contract line plus wire).

**The parent is now the frontier, and it was not before.** The first build of this codec
wrapped `anadiplosisEncode`, three layers below the codec the repository actually ships.
That is a measured difference, not a naming quibble: on `dump.sql` the inner layer returns
662 where `ouraEncode` returns 608, on `page.html` 561 against 547, on `vix` 582 against
579, on `find-listing` 1 279 against 1 267. A gate against the inner layer would let
APOGRAPHE claim a win over a codec nobody ships, so every number in this report was
**retaken after the parent was switched to `ouraEncode`** (§7.1). The deltas above are
parent-relative and therefore honest; they are also 2–3 tokens larger than the first,
wrong-parent build reported.

## 1. The mechanism

### 1.1 Why every codec before this one pays a tax

CHIRON, ANADIPLOSIS, SYNCOPE, PYKNOSIS, SYMPLOKE, LAKONIKOS, TIMEMA, EUTeleia and OURA are
all **substitution** codecs. Each finds a span and writes something shorter where the span
was. A substitution needs a **pointer at the point of substitution** — a one-token glyph if
you are lucky, a count plus framing if you are not. That pointer is a per-use tax, and it is
why each of them is gated on "count ≥ 3 before it pays": with one token for the glyph and the
rule text in the tape, a rule used twice costs more than it saves.

That is not a law of compression. It is a law of *substitution*, and it stops applying when
the position of a value is already determined by the document.

### 1.2 The observation

A record set is a matrix with two coordinate systems:

* the **field order inside a line** — field *k* is field *k* on every line;
* the **line order in the file** — line *i* is the record after line *i−1*.

A reader told the shape of the matrix gets both coordinates for free. A column whose value is
a function of the line above therefore does not have to be written at all, and no pointer is
needed where it would have gone, because the reader knows *where* to put it: at field *k*,
which the arity of the other lines identifies.

### 1.3 The wire

```
Before ¶ k=v. expand; print only.                    ← the parent's contract (11 tokens)
§ …tape… ¶ …payload…                                 ← the parent's wire
<2|,|1=workdays>                                     ← APOGRAPHE's header, the payload's line 1
2014-01-02,77.44539475
77.04557544                                          ← 1 field, not 2: column 1 is lifted
2014-01-06,74.89697204                               ← 2 fields: a literal row, which resets
```

The header `<n<HSEP>sep<HSEP>col=model<HSEP>…>` declares, once:

* `n` — the full arity of a source line;
* `sep` — the field separator (one character, and it may not be the header separator);
* one `col=model` per **lifted** column (1-based), where the model is one of
  `above` (repeat the line above), `colM` (copy column M of the same line),
  `days` (next calendar day), `workdays` (next weekday, so Friday advances by three),
  `+b` / `−b` (the number above plus/minus b, re-rendered at the same digit width).

The reader disambiguates **by arithmetic, not by markers**:

* a line with `n` fields is **literal** — copied out, and it **resets** every predictor;
* a line with `n − L` fields (`L` = number of lifted columns) is **modelled** — missing
  fields rebuilt, kept fields consumed in order;
* any other field count is copied verbatim and the state is untouched.

That is the whole format. No exception list, no position vector, no escape code, no second
message.

### 1.4 Self-synchronisation (why it is exact *and* cheap)

An exception is not patched; it is **written in full**, and writing a full line resets the
state. After any break the reader and the writer agree again by construction, and the encoder
never has to enumerate, quote or index exceptions. On `aapl-2014.csv` that is 9 literal rows
out of 241 — the NYSE holidays and two half-days — at about 4 tokens each, against 232
modelled rows at about 4.3 tokens each *without* their date.

This is the classical escape-code / exception design of frame-of-reference and delta coding
in column stores (Abadi, Madden & Ferreira, SIGMOD 2006; ALP, SIGMOD 2024), *with the
difference that the escape is the unencoded value itself and the resynchronising state is the
arity of the line rather than an escape symbol in a bit stream.* No chat reader can decode a
bit-packed exception vector; it can decode this.

### 1.5 Greedy or not

**Greedy core, exact gate.** The planner adds one (column, predictor) pair at a time, keeping
only additions that strictly reduce the measured token count of the transformed text; it never
backtracks and never enumerates subsets. The transformed text is then run through the **whole
frontier stack**, and the result ships only if the measured one-chat message is strictly
smaller than the frontier's own message. So the search is greedy; the decision to fire is an
exact comparison of two whole messages.

### 1.6 Time and CPU bound

Yes, and bounded twice. The planner costs at most `arity` rounds of at most `arity × 6`
candidate transforms; each candidate costs one `apographeApply` (a single left-to-right pass)
plus one token count, plus one `apographeUndo` **soundness check before the measurement**. The
expensive part is the single extra frontier encode of the transformed text, and that encode is
cheaper than the frontier's encode of the source on every lane where it fires, because the
transformed text is shorter. Measured on the current build: `psql` 22 s against the frontier's
22 s, `vix` 267 s against 176 s **worse** (the extra encode of the transformed text does not
pay back on that lane because the transform removes row separators the frontier was exploiting
cheaply), `aapl` 295 s against 219 s **worse** for the same reason. The time bound is real;
the *speed* is not improved, and it is worse on the two big firing lanes. Stated because it is
easy to hide.

## 2. Exactness: what is proved and what is only tested

**Invariants the implementation maintains** (enforced in code, not by convention):

1. `apographeParseHeader(renderHeader(cfg)) ≡ cfg` for every configuration the encoder can
   emit — the writer parses its own header and refuses the plan if the parse does not
   reproduce the configuration.
2. `apographeApply` and `apographeUndo` both walk columns `0…n−1`, so a `colM` predictor sees
   the same values in both directions.
3. A modelled-width line whose prediction is not exactly the observed text makes
   `apographeApply` return `null` (refusal), never a corrupt line.
4. Soundness is checked **before** the measurement: `apographeUndo(apply(x)) === x` gates every
   candidate, so a non-invertible candidate can never win on tokens.
5. `apographeUndo` returns `null` for anything that is not a well-formed wire; the decoder then
   returns the parent's expansion unchanged rather than guessing.

**What is not proved.** No machine-checked proof of 1–5 exists. They are properties of a
~900-line TypeScript module plus a ~400-line independent re-implementation, both tested (§3).
There is no theorem prover in this sandbox and nothing here is claimed to be formalised.

**Why they are enough.** The header is self-describing and each line's arity determines its
kind, so decoding is a deterministic single left-to-right pass with no search. The only
ambiguity that can exist is a **source line that already has `n − L` fields**; that case is
excluded by construction — `apographeApply` returns `null` if any source line has `n − L`
fields, and the decoder refuses rather than guesses.

## 3. Verification performed this turn

Everything below was executed here; nothing is projected.

### 3.1 Red team, 43/43 (`bench/x39/rt.ts`)

`apographeApply` → `apographeUndo` on hand-built and seeded-random inputs, asserting the only
two allowed outcomes: *refused* (`null`) or *byte-exact*. Re-run after every repair below.

* calendar primitives: `2000-01-01` = JDN 2451545 is a Saturday; 2014-01-03 (Friday) + next
  workday = 2014-01-06; 2016-02-28 + 1 day = 02-29; 1900 is not a leap year, 2000 is; invalid
  dates and wrong widths rejected;
* structure: CRLF, no trailing newline, leap-year run, year rollover, ragged rows, blank lines,
  mixed EOLs, pipe-separated with spaces, tab-separated, negative numbers, zero-padded
  counters, `above` with an empty field, chained `colM`, a date model on a non-date column
  (never fires), a width-overflowing counter (goes literal);
* refusals: a source that already has `n−L` fields; a document whose first line starts with the
  header opener;
* header grammar: parse of `<2|,|1=workdays>` and `<5;|;2=+1;3=col2>`; rejection of a
  right-pointing `colM`, a duplicate column, an out-of-range column, an unknown model, an empty
  lift list, `n = 1`;
* **seeded fuzz, 300 random record sets**: 223 transformed byte-exact, 77 refused, 0 corrupt.
  Refusal is a correct outcome on the arity-ambiguity and prediction-failure paths.

### 3.2 An independent decoder, written from the contract prose, in another language

`bench/x39/apographe_decode.py` is a CPython reader that imports nothing from this repository
except `bench/chiron_decode.py`, itself written from the wire description. It re-derives the
header grammar, the predictor arithmetic and the self-synchronising state machine from what
the message *says*, then decodes the messages of the **current** build:

```
$ python3 bench/x39/apographe_decode.py bench/x39/receipts/smoke4.json /tmp/out4.json
EXACT    aapl-2014.csv
EXACT    vix-daily-1990.csv
EXACT    psql-output.txt
```

This is the strongest mechanically checkable form of the "a model can read it" claim: the
format survives re-implementation by a reader that has never seen the TypeScript, with no
shared code, and recovers all three sources byte for byte.

### 3.2b A hand-decode receipt (third implementation, in the message's own terms)

`bench/x39/hand_decode.py` carries one complete message verbatim — **168 tokens, 382 bytes**, a
41-line CSV of 40 business days starting 2016-02-25 with a counting quantity column — and
reconstructs the **682-byte** source from the message text alone, using only what the contract
sentence says. No import of the repository, no access to encoder state, and a different
implementation style again:

```
$ python3 bench/x39/hand_decode.py
hand-decode exact: True | rows 41 | bytes 682
```

The wire is small enough to read: `<3|,|2=workdays|3=+1>`, then the CSV's own header line, then
one literal data row (`1,2016-02-25,40`) that establishes the state, then 39 lines containing
nothing but the row number (`2`, `3`, … `40`). Everything else — every date and every quantity —
is regenerated by the reader: the next weekday after the date above, and the number above plus
one at the same width. That is the mechanism in one screen: **`2` is a whole row.**

Writing that reader was itself a test of the contract text. It found three real ambiguities,
now fixed on both sides:

1. the shape clause (`8 digits become YYYY-MM-DD`) is inherited from the parent and is **not
   delimited** — the sentence runs `…YYYY-MM-DD Header line = n…`, so a reader that splits on
   whitespace or punctuation mis-parses it. Every clause must be found by an independent
   pattern match;
2. the header separator and the column separator are different characters, and the header
   declares which is which only by position (`<7;|;…>`: header sep `;`, column sep `|`);
3. `colN=+b` is width-preserving, so `user_0007 → user_0008` and `999 → 1000` are *not* the
   same rule; a reader that renders `1000` where the source had `999` destroys exactness, which
   is why the model re-renders at the same digit count and refuses otherwise.

### 3.4 A new lane falsified the *harness*, not the encoder — and it is fixed

`holdout-lang/pl-kb.txt` (Polish, 2 096 chars) failed the repo's own round-trip check — 602
tokens, `MISMATCH` — under the decode call used by `bench/x38/verify.ts`:

```ts
undo: r.parent === 'identity' ? (w) => w : chironDecode
```

On that lane `r.parent === 'identity'`, so the harness passes the identity function — but the
wire **is** a CHIRON tape wire (it opens with `§`). Decoding it with `chironDecode`
unconditionally is **EXACT**:

```
pl-kb.txt      parent=identity  labelArm=FAIL  tapeArm=EXACT   wireStartsWithTape=true
ru-kb.txt      parent=identity  labelArm=EXACT tapeArm=EXACT   wireStartsWithTape=false
kb-article.txt parent=lakonikos labelArm=EXACT tapeArm=EXACT   wireStartsWithTape=true
```

The defect is an **arm-label / decode-guard inconsistency**: `parent === 'identity'` does not
imply "the wire has no tape", and the label is not part of the message at all, so no
third-party reader can use it. APOGRAPHE now decides the arm **from the wire**
(`apographeArmOf`: a wire opening with `§` and containing `¶` carries rules) and `pl-kb`
round-trips byte-exact in the corrected build. The parent's own harness still has the
label-based rule; that line should be fixed before the next frontier table is trusted at
17/17.

## 4. The channel-capacity audit — what is actually left

This is the part of the turn that changes what the next turn should do, and it is measured.

**(a) The digit channel — 9.97 bits/token, measured.** Random decimal digits, 300 / 3 000 /
30 000 of them: exactly 3.000 digits per o200k token every time, i.e. 9.97 bits per token.
o200k's digit merges are the densest *declarable* alphabet in the tokenizer.

**(b) Any printable-character alphabet is below that.** One token per character, at most
log2(95) = 6.57 bits/token for ASCII printables. So for numbers, **no substitution into single
characters can beat ordinary digits** — which retroactively explains OURA's measured zero, the
digit-run remapping negatives of X34 (#4, #5) and base64 as a target (X34 #8). Any future
proposal of the form "pack the number into denser characters" is refuted by this measurement
before it is written.

**(c) The word channel beats digits only through the reader's knowledge.** Two thousand *random*
six-letter words cost 3.3 tokens each (measured), so the `log2 K` bits/token of an atom alphabet
only exist for strings that are actually in the vocabulary — and a reader cannot enumerate "the
30 000 one-token words of o200k in lexicographic order". The word channel is therefore usable
only through the reader's knowledge of English, which is what the parent already exploits.

**The instrument.** `pb = 8 · brotli-11(source) / wire tokens` — how many bits of a strong
general-purpose compressor's output each wire token stands in for. It is a *density ratio*, not
an entropy claim (brotli overshoots entropy, so `pb` can exceed any channel cap), but it orders
lanes correctly and assumes nothing:

| regime | lanes (`pb`) | reading |
|---|---|---|
| prose | kb-article 14.5, meeting 14.2, email 13.4, llm-answer 12.6, biblio 12.4 | at the word channel's ceiling: **nothing left for a substitution codec** |
| delimited tables | aapl 10.5, vix 8.1, psql 8.5, df-h 7.6, kubectl 9.3, markdown-table 5.9 | **below the digit ceiling** — the remaining loss is *structure* |
| ops / markup | dump 8.8, page 9.8, chart 9.5, npm-ls 11.6, find-listing 11.0, openstack 11.3, numstat 10.8 | mixed; timestamps dominate the sub-10 lanes |

Conclusion: the next win is **not** another substitution codec and **not** a denser alphabet
(there is none declarable). It is more *structure removal* on the lanes where `pb < 10`:
date/time columns of ops files, and the separators and layout of tables. APOGRAPHE took the
delimited-table direction and moved two lanes by 20–30 %; the ops direction (irregular
timestamps on `find-listing`, `openstack`, `git-numstat`) is still open at `pb` ≈ 9–11.

## 5. Negative space measured this turn (16 shapes)

1. **Lifting the value column of `aapl`** — refused by the planner: prices are 8–10 significant
   decimals with no cross-row arithmetic relation, so the predictor never fires and the literal
   fallback costs more than the 4.3 tokens/row it already costs.
2. **`days` instead of `workdays` on `vix`** — rejected by measurement (business-daily series:
   every Monday would go literal).
3. **`+b` on the `aapl` date column** — cannot fire: dates are not single digit runs.
4. **A second header delimiter form** (`<n|sep|col=model;…>`) — **unsound**: the parser rejected
   its own output when `sep` equalled the header separator. Removed.
5. **Predicting modelled columns before kept fields** — **unsound** for `colM`: the model read a
   column that had not been written yet. Fixed by walking `0…n−1`.
6. **Majority-vote column proposals** — produced `NO PLAN` on every lane: one title row vetoed
   the whole column. Removed.
7. **A clause that does not say "resets"** — 4–6 tokens cheaper, **unsound**: the reader cannot
   know that a literal line re-synchronises the predictors. Refused on principle.
8. **Omitting the header's arity `n`** — the reader cannot tell literal from modelled; 1 token
   saved, exactness lost.
9. **Digit-stream transposition of the numeric column** — the floor it approaches is 3.33
   tokens/row on `aapl` against the measured 4.15, so at most 20 % on one lane, and it needs the
   reader to re-split a 2 320-character digit stream by a width list: an error mode that cannot
   be verified inside the message.
10. **A generated trading calendar** ("business days of 2014 minus these 9 dates") — worth ~300
    more tokens on `aapl`, but the reader must compute day-of-week for arbitrary dates, a new
    silent failure mode with no in-wire check. Held.
11. **Base-676 (`aa`…`zz`) as a denser number alphabet** — 9.4 bits/token at two letters, below
    the measured 9.97 of plain digits. Negative by arithmetic.
12. **TOON-style key-once tabular form** — the parent already does the equivalent inside a
    line-oriented wire; TOON's published gains are relative to JSON, which repeats keys.
13. **Exception vectors with positions (ALP-style)** — the point of APOGRAPHE is that a full
    line carries its own position; a position vector costs more than the 9 literal rows it
    would replace on `aapl`.
14. **Variable arity** — the reader must know `n` before it can tell literal from modelled;
    variable arity re-introduces the ambiguity the format rests on.
15. **APOGRAPHE inside the tape** — the tape is a delimiter-free rule list, not a record set;
    no arity to key on. Zero by construction.
16. **A learned `date+s` (weekly / monthly) model** — implemented in the plan space but not
    shipped: no lane in the corpus has a strided calendar that `workdays`/`days` does not
    already cover, so it would have been untested code.
17. **`git-numstat` (the important refusal)** — the planner's proxy proposed `<3;|;2=above>` and
    predicted a **725-token** gain on raw text (3 859 → 725). The frontier's own encode of the
    transformed text came out at **1 992, unchanged**: the parent was already harvesting that
    column more cheaply than a lifted column plus header could. The whole-message gate refused,
    correctly. Proxy gains on raw text are not wins, and this lane is the calibration case.

## 6. Branch report

### 6.1 APOGRAPHE (shipped this turn)

* **Central construction.** A record set is a matrix; the arity of a line encodes which
  columns are present; a one-line header names a one-step predictor per lifted column; a
  literal full-arity line carries its value *and* resets the predictors, so the format is
  self-synchronising and exception-free.
* **Concrete artifact.** `src/lib/omega/apographe.ts` (encode / plan / apply / undo / header
  parse+render / arm detection), registry key `apographe`, `bench/x39/{rt.ts, receipts.ts,
  emit.ts, apographe_decode.py, audit.ts, parts.ts, dbg*.ts}`, `bench/x39/receipts/*.jsonl`.
* **Proved portion.** Header/render round trip, ordered apply/undo, refusal on arity ambiguity,
  soundness-before-measurement — invariants 1–5, tested 43/43 plus a from-spec Python decoder.
* **Exact unresolved interface.** The reader's *arithmetic*: `workdays` requires the reader to
  know which weekday a date is, and nothing in the message checks it. A reader that miscounts a
  weekday produces a wrong date with no in-wire signal.
* **Cheapest falsification test.** Encode a lane whose dates cross a leap year *and* a century
  non-leap year (1900, 2100) with `workdays`, and require the independent reader to stay exact.
  `rt.ts` covers 1900 and 2016 at the primitive level; no real lane does.
* **Local or fundamental?** Local. A representation choice for one class of input; it does not
  touch the prose plateau, and §4 explains why nothing will.

### 6.2 The prose plateau (not attacked this turn; X33–X38 did)

* **Central construction.** Substitution with a token-measured gate.
* **Proved portion (measured).** Prose body ≈ distinct-word token count, so the tape cannot pay;
  14.2–14.5 bits/token against a 14.9 ceiling.
* **Exact unresolved interface.** The reader's knowledge of English. Every mechanism that would
  exploit it (semantic projection, BabelTele-style collapse, LLM-as-arithmetic-coder) is either
  lossy or needs the reader to reproduce probabilities exactly, which no one-message format can
  guarantee.
* **Cheapest falsification test.** Hand-write the shortest byte-exact one-message encoding of one
  prose lane; if it is ≥ 90 % of the parent's M, the plateau holds for human encoders too. Not run.
* **Local or fundamental?** Fundamental for this architecture.

### 6.3 Strided timestamps for ops lanes (not shipped, highest expected value next)

* **Central construction.** A declared stride class per timestamp column (fixed step, or an
  enumerated set of common strides) with literal resynchronisation, exactly as APOGRAPHE does
  for dates.
* **Proved portion.** Only the economics: `pb` 8.8–11.3 on four ops lanes, and HORA's `※` mark
  shows a per-row delta pointer costs ~1 token where these lanes spend ~2.5–3 on their
  timestamps.
* **Exact unresolved interface.** Irregular inter-arrival times: a distribution cannot be sent
  losslessly for less than the times themselves unless it is a small enumerated stride set.
* **Cheapest falsification test.** Per ops lane, the fraction of consecutive timestamps whose
  difference is in {0, 1 ms, 1 s, 1 min, 1 h, 1 day} and the token cost of the exceptions. If the
  modal stride covers < 60 % of transitions, the direction dies. One hour, not run this turn.
* **Local or fundamental?** Local and cheap.

### 6.4 Seven mechanism-distinct approaches, each with its structural adversary A(C)

`A(C)` is the cheapest *input class* that makes candidate `C` either wrong or a no-op — not a
bug report, a shape of data. Only approach 1 is shipped this turn; the rest are ranked by
expected value in §8.

| 5 | **Column-major streaming**: transpose the table, compress each column as a run, send a layout header | transpose is bijective; row order is recoverable from lengths | field boundaries inside a column when values have variable length | transpose `aapl`, compare the run encode against the row-major wire | values containing the **separator** (quoted CSV): column boundaries become text-dependent, so the layout header must describe quoting — the classic CSV trap |
| 6 | **Strided timestamp generator for logs** | economics only: `pb` 8.8–11.3 on four ops lanes | irregular inter-arrival distributions | the modal-stride coverage test of §6.3 on `find-listing` | a **bursty** log (millisecond bursts separated by seconds): no single stride covers 60 %, so the exceptions are the payload |
| 7 | **Arithmetic coding with the reader's own prior** (the strongest known compressor, Delétang et al.) | theorem: code length = −log p under a fixed model | the decoder is a chat model; it cannot reproduce probabilities exactly | ask the message to carry the model's probability updates — impossible in one message without a decoder program | **any** text: the adversary is the interface itself, not the data (this is why it is not attempted, not why it failed) |

**What this table says that the measurements do not.** Four of the seven candidates (2, 3, 5, 6)
fail on the *same* adversary class — data whose regularity is not exactly reproducible by a
reader with only the message in front of it. That is the real constraint of this whole
programme: not the tokenizer, and not the algorithm, but **what a reader can be trusted to
compute**. Approach 1 is the only one in the list whose computation is a lookahead of one row
and a weekday increment.

## 7. Repairs made this turn (and the trust they do not inherit)

### 7.1 The parent was the wrong codec (the big one)

`apographeEncode` was gated against `anadiplosisEncode`, three codecs below the frontier. This
was invisible on 13 of 17 lanes and worth **−54 on `dump.sql`, −14 on `page.html`, −3 on `vix`,
−12 on `find-listing`** — in the wrong direction, i.e. it would have let APOGRAPHE claim wins
against a codec nobody ships. Found by comparing re-measured parent numbers with the X38 table
rather than trusting either. Fixed: the parent is `ouraEncode`, the decoder is `ouraDecode`, all
measurements retaken (§0), and the fix is documented at the import site so the next codec does
not repeat it.

### 7.2 A writer that could not be read

The first version could emit a header whose separator collided with the field separator
(`<4|||2=above>`), which its own parser cannot read back. Found by the from-spec reader. Fixed
twice: the header separator is chosen to differ from the field separator, **and** the writer now
parses its own header and refuses the plan if the parse does not reproduce the configuration.

### 7.3 Unsound column ordering

`colM` could be planned for a column before the column it copies was written. Both directions now
walk columns `0…n−1` and record kept values into the row before any predictor reads them; the red
team case `copy of an earlier column` exists because of this bug.

### 7.4 Arm detection from the wire, not from a label

See §3.4. `apographeArmOf(wire)` replaces `parent === 'identity'`, and `pl-kb` — which failed the
parent's harness — round-trips in the corrected build.

Each repair was followed by a full re-run of the red team and of every quoted measurement. A
repaired candidate inherits no trust; none of the numbers above predate §7.1.

## 9. Prior art, honestly

**The mechanism is not new to databases; it is new to one-chat, model-readable formats.**

* **Frame-of-reference and delta coding with escape-coded exceptions, executed on compressed
  columns**: Abadi, Madden & Ferreira, *Integrating Compression and Execution in Column-Oriented
  Database Systems*, SIGMOD 2006, pp. 671–682 (doi:10.1145/1142473.1142548). The direct ancestor
  of `+b` and of the literal-line-as-escape design.
* **Out-of-band exception vectors patched on decode**: Afroozeh, Kuffó & Boncz, *ALP: Adaptive
  Lossless floating-Point Compression*, SIGMOD 2024 (doi:10.1145/3626717); *G-ALP*, 2025
  (doi:10.1145/3736227.3736242). ALP's decimal transform is what `+b` does for a fixed-precision
  money column; its exception vector is what APOGRAPHE deliberately does not need.
* **Taxonomy**: Damme et al., *Lightweight Data Compression Algorithms: An Experimental Survey*,
  EDBT 2017 — FOR, DELTA, DICT, RLE, null suppression.
* **Key-declared-once tabular text**: TOON (*Token-Oriented Object Notation*, `toonformat.dev`,
  GitHub `toon-format/toon`) declares the field list once per array and streams one row per
  value; its published benchmark is 42.6 % fewer tokens than JSON at equal retrieval accuracy.
  This is the nearest published neighbour to APOGRAPHE's header and it is a **different
  mechanism**: TOON removes repeated *keys*, APOGRAPHE removes repeated *values*. Independent
  benchmark of format switching with accuracy costs: *Notation Matters*, arXiv:2605.29676
  (TOON −18 %, TRON −27 %, accuracy −1…−9 pp).
* **Model-readable rather than human-readable encodings**: *Large Language Models Do Not Always
  Need Readable Language* (BabelTele), arXiv:2606.19857 — 27.9 % of original length at 99.5 %
  semantic fidelity. **Semantic, not byte-exact**, and cited here as the boundary we refuse to
  cross.
* **Language models as compressors** (the resource deliberately not spent): Delétang et al.,
  *Language Modeling Is Compression*, ICLR 2024, arXiv:2309.10668 — arithmetic coding with the
  model as predictor; the decoder must reproduce the model's probabilities exactly, which is why
  a bit stream cannot be read out of a chat message.

**Claims about AI mathematics found this turn (searched, not verified here).** OpenAI's October
2026 release of 722 manuscripts in 372 result families, with Lean formalisations for "about 42 %"
of top-level results and 3 withdrawals (secondary sources); Anthropic's Lean 4 formalisation of
Fermat's Last Theorem, ~13 M lines, reported September 2026; the May 2026 Erdős unit-distance
disproof with a Lean certificate and an arXiv write-up by nine mathematicians; Shanghai AI Lab's
Ghys-conjecture formalisation, October 2026. **None of this is evidence for anything in this
report**, and none of it was verified by me — there is no Lean toolchain in this sandbox and I
did not fetch the repositories. The procedural lesson is the one that matters here: a certificate
a third party can run is worth more than a description of a result, which is why §3.2 exists.
