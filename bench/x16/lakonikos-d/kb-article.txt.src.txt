Understanding Retention Policies in Northgate Vault

A retention policy determines how long Northgate Vault keeps a document before it becomes
eligible for permanent deletion. Every document inherits exactly one retention policy, and that
policy is resolved at the moment the document is created. Changing a policy later affects only
documents created after the change; it never retroactively alters the disposition date of a
document that already exists. This behaviour is deliberate, because retroactive changes would
make it impossible to prove to an auditor what the disposition schedule was at any given point
in the past.

Policies are evaluated in a fixed order. The system first looks for a policy attached directly to
the document. If none exists, it looks for a policy attached to the folder that contains the
document, then to that folder's parent, and so on up to the workspace root. If no policy is found
anywhere on that path, the workspace default applies. The workspace default cannot be removed,
only changed, which guarantees that every document always resolves to some policy.

There are three kinds of disposition. Soft deletion moves the document to a recycle bin where it
remains visible to administrators for a further thirty days. Hard deletion removes the document
content but keeps an immutable audit stub recording that a document existed, who created it, and
when it was disposed. Purge removes everything, including the audit stub, and is only available
to workspaces operating under a legal regime that requires it; most customers should never enable
purge, because it destroys the evidence that the retention policy was followed.

A legal hold suspends all disposition for the documents it covers. Holds take precedence over
every policy, including purge, and they cannot be overridden by an administrator. Only a user with
the Legal Hold Officer role may place or release a hold, and every placement and release is
written to the audit log with the reason supplied by that user. When a hold is released, the
affected documents return to their normal schedule; if their disposition date has already passed,
they become eligible immediately and will be processed in the next nightly sweep.

The nightly sweep runs at 02:00 in the workspace's configured timezone. It processes documents in
creation order, oldest first, and stops after four hours regardless of how much work remains.
Anything not processed is picked up by the next sweep. Because the sweep is time-bounded rather
than work-bounded, a workspace with a very large backlog may take several nights to catch up, and
administrators sometimes mistake this for a failure. The sweep report, available under Reports and
Audit, shows exactly how many documents were considered, how many were disposed, and how many
remained in the queue when the sweep stopped.

Two questions come up repeatedly in support tickets. The first is why a document with an expired
disposition date is still visible. Almost always the answer is either an active legal hold or a
backlogged sweep, both of which the sweep report will show. The second is whether a retention
policy can be shortened. It can, but only for future documents, and the interface deliberately
makes this awkward in order to prevent an administrator from accidentally believing that existing
documents have been reshuffled onto the shorter schedule.
