## Compression papers and systems (within 20 years)

- Masui et al., *A compression scheme for radio data in high performance computing* (2015), [1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.00638). Fetched first PDF chunk, search excerpt identifies bit-plane matrix transposition. The abstract's 28% result combines a nearly-lossless rounding step with lossless Bitshuffle/HDF5. The rounding result is inadmissible here. Only the exact permutation idea transfers; speed/ratio on typed radio arrays does not transfer to o200k text or unaided LLM reading. PSIFIDA uses decimal-character positions and explicit text holes, not bits or machine SIMD.
- Delaunay, Courtois, Gouillon, *Evaluation of lossless and lossy algorithms for the compression of scientific datasets in netCDF-4 or HDF5 files* (2019), DOI 10.5194/gmd-12-4099-2019, [3](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/12/4099/2019/). Fetched abstract/introduction. Shuffle/Bitshuffle separate preprocessing from compression. Their digit rounding is explicitly error-bounded loss, so it must not be imported into a byte-exact codec. Mixed lossy/lossless benchmark headlines are not exact-compression evidence.
- *Tokenization Is More Than Compression* (EMNLP 2024), [1](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.40.pdf), search excerpt only. PathPiece assumes a chosen vocabulary containing all 256 byte tokens and optimizes segmentation. Our target uses fixed o200k tokenization; choosing a cheaper segmentation cannot change what the model's tokenizer actually emits.
- *The Functionalizer* (arXiv 2609.15991), [3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15991), search excerpt only, date metadata inconsistent with arXiv identifier. It factors orthographic variants into PUA opcode streams; reported smaller vocabularies coexist with longer sequences. Neither a new vocabulary nor reduced vocabulary size meets this request. Do not assert the retrieved date as established.
- Tufazip (2026), [5](https://tufalabs.ai/research/tufazip/), fetched first of five page chunks. Author reports 105,388,353 bytes for enwik9 including decoder/weights. That system uses deterministic neural prediction and arithmetic coding; its executable decoder and model training are outside our one-chat native access model. The useful lesson is charging *all* decoder side information, not claiming this ratio for readable messages.

### OpenAI: bounded-width exact SAT counting
Freshly fetched `lean/ComparatorChallenges/SubexponentialSAT.lean` at reported repository head `4eaee87548250b2682affda7d05d947c05554bae`. The inspected theorem quantifies `for each k >= 2, exists a primitive machine, tapes, positive constants and degrees, for every n and width-k CNF ...`. Exact count and decision are both required, output count has <=n+1 bits, and visited-space is bounded at **every execution prefix**, not only the final configuration. Writing L for the actual formula encoding length, the inspected runtime is C(L+1)^d * 2^(A n (ln ln(n+16))² / sqrt(ln(n+16))); constants/degrees can depend on k. Formula encoding length also appears in the polynomial visited-space bound. The squared log-log and natural logarithms are part of this statement; it is not identical to the Anthropic bound below. The inherited executable encoding audit is rerun here (2,066 finite instances); it checks only encoding lengths, not the counting algorithm. This is stronger specification discipline than a final-output or final-space check, but different from a single machine chosen before k. The `sorry` is in the challenge statement, not evidence that the submitted solution has a proof hole. No concrete practical compressor or SAT executable was extracted. Reducing a bounded grammar search to SAT would additionally need a correct reduction, token-objective encoding, finite constants, actual solver, and native decoding gate; this result alone supplies none of those.

### Axiom: Lyons–White
Fresh repository head, `Challenge.lean`, and its imported `Challenge/Basic.lean` fetched at head `0cd9e3b1c5335869b304924d51bbba39327a17e9`. The definition compares all symmetric nonnegative generating rate collections lambda<=mu at every t>=0, using distance of the heat distribution from uniform. The forward statement is for finite dihedral groups D_n with n>=1 and even p=2m, m>=1. Its generalization is to inversion extensions of finite abelian A with z+z=0. The converse says for each real p>1 not an even positive integer there exists a dihedral group failing monotonicity; it does NOT cover p=1. The heat matrix is exp(-t L(laplacian)), evaluated at the identity column, with real part used in the real distribution. These definitions align with the earlier heat-kernel source inspection, but the challenge contains statement placeholders rather than the submitted proof. No kernel replay occurred. No argument has been established connecting this specific random-walk norm monotonicity to shorter messages or native readers. ## Decision
